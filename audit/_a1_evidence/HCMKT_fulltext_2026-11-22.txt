 a b s t r a c t
Knowledge tracing aims to track the evolution of knowledge state based on student’s historical learning records 
to predict future performance, which has become a key part of intelligent tutoring systems. Existing methods 
typically model the learning process based on structural relationships between questions or skills, but the extrac-
tion of multi-granularity heterogeneous features from sparse learning records has not been suﬃciently explored. 
In this paper, we propose a heterogeneous graph knowledge tracing method (HCMKT) with novel contrastive 
view and meta-path contexts to solve the above problem. Speciﬁcally, we employ meta-paths to construct coarse 
and ﬁne-grained views, capturing higher-order interaction features among questions. Then, to learn high-quality 
question representations, we construct contrastive view between the coarse and ﬁne-grained views with an elab-
orate negative sample selection mechanism. Additionally, diverse question-answer interaction representations 
are input into the LSTM network for state modeling. Finally, we integrate coarse and ﬁne-grained modeling 
processes using online knowledge distillation to enhance model robustness. Extensive experiments conducted on 
four datasets demonstrate the rationality and eﬀectiveness of HCMKT.
1.  Introduction
With the vigorous development of Artiﬁcial Intelligence (AI) technol-
ogy, exploring how to fully empower AI in various ﬁelds has received 
unprecedented attention. Computer-aided learning is an important em-
bodiment of AI in education, playing a pivotal role in tailored educa-
tion. However, how to mine meaningful student learning characteristics 
from massive educational data to provide personalized education has 
become a pressing issue. As a result, knowledge tracing (KT) (Abdelrah-
man et al., 2023; Corbett & Anderson, 1994; Liu et al., 2019), which aims 
to assess student’s future performance based on their historical learning 
data, has therefore received considerable attention by researchers in re-
cent years. Speciﬁcally, the KT modeling mechanism of this paper is 
shown in Fig. 1.
KT, a prominent ﬁeld within educational data mining, has produced 
numerous high-performing models, encompassing traditional theories 
(Corbett, 2000; Kasurinen & Nikula, 2009) and deep learning (DL) 
paradigms (Ke et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2023; Song 
et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2024). Currently, main-
stream KT models typically embed input factors as dense numerical vec-
tor representations before network prediction (Zhang et al., 2023). The

ties (such as question, skill and templated) in graphs. In these methods, 
the correspondences between entities are usually skill-to-skill, question-
to-question, and skill-to-question. Through capturing similar feature in-
formation between skills or questions on the graph to enrich the in-
put representation, the robustness of modeling and prediction is en-

tionships between skills or questions, while ignoring the heterogeneous 
information between questions, may limit information gain. There-
fore, a question-centered heterogeneous information network (HIN) is 
constructed in HCMKT, and the meta-path view with question-entity-
question as path instances is established. Unlike KT studies introducing 
meta-paths, which only considered connectivity, we incorporated their 
semantic context and treated the meta-path set formed by question at-
tributes as meta-path contexts. Speciﬁcally, by introducing a relay en-
tity to connect questions, HCMKT capture higher-order heterogeneous 
relationships among questions at a deeper level, while integrating more 
feature nodes at a broader scale.
Self-supervised learning (SSL) (Yu et al., 2024) enables models 
to learn high-quality representations by deriving supervisory signals 
from data. Among various SSL methods, contrastive learning (CL) has 
emerged as a representative approach and has garnered signiﬁcant at-
tention in KT. In KT, sequential data augmentation strategies (such 
as response ﬂipping and data replacement) are primarily employed 
to generate contrastive view for improving representation learning 
(Dai et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2023). Although these 
approaches improve model performance, they often overlook hetero-
geneous attribute information. Furthermore, they struggle to capture 
higher-order semantic relationships among entities such as questions, 
skills, and assignments. Introducing CL in KT enhances the model’s capa-
bility to discern feature similarities in high-dimensional representation 
spaces, eﬀectively capturing deep semantic relationships among enti-
ties. This improves the tracking of knowledge state evolution. There-
fore, we construct contrastive view based on question representations 
from coarse and ﬁne-grained modeling, enabling CL to capture macro-
level structural information and micro-level semantic features. More-
over, as knowledge distillation (KD) enables real-time learning and con-
tinuous reﬁnement, HCMKT leverages KD to integrate modeling net-
works across diﬀerent views, improving its robustness and predictive
accuracy.
In this paper, to adequately extract multi-granularity heterogeneous 
features from sparse learning records, we propose a heterogeneous 
graph knowledge tracing method (HCMKT) with novel contrastive view 
and meta-path contexts. A coarse view and a ﬁne-grained view are con-
structed leveraging meta-paths to characterize heterogeneous relation-
ships among questions. The coarse view indicates that two question 
objects are connected via meta-paths, while the ﬁne-grained view de-
scribes the semantic details of their connections based on the meta-path 
contexts. By constructing these two views, HCMKT not only integrates 
node features on a broader scale, but also captures the heterogeneous re-
lationships between questions on a deeper level. To learn high-quality 
question representations, we not only simply aggregate by meta-path 
induced graphs to generate node embeddings, but also introduce noise 
into the ﬁne-grained view for graph augmentation to generate node 
embeddings again. We further apply special negative sample selection 
mechanisms and prototype contrastive learning approach to augment 
the learning ability. By constructing contrastive view between coarse 
and ﬁne-grained views, HCMKT is able to capture macro-level struc-
tural information and micro-level semantic features, eﬀectively model-
ing higher-order heterogeneous semantic relationships between ques-
tions. Moreover, to accurately predict student’s future performance, the 
question-answer interaction representations are fed into Long Short-
Term Memory (LSTM) for knowledge state modeling. Finally. we com-
bine the learning process in coarse and ﬁne-grained views by online 
knowledge distillation to improve the model’s modeling, prediction, and 
generalization performance. The key contributions are summarized as:
∙ We propose a novel heterogeneous graph knowledge tracing method, 
HCMKT, which extracts multi-granularity heterogeneous features by 
constructing a coarse view and a ﬁne-grained view from sparse learn-
ing records.
∙ We utilize contrastive learning and an elaborated negative sample se-
lection mechanism to eﬀectively capture macro-level structural in-
formation and micro-level semantic features, enhancing HCMKT’s 
capability to learn high-quality question representations.
∙ We employ an online knowledge distillation mechanism to jointly 
co-optimize the modeling of coarse and ﬁne-grained views, which 
improves the model’s representation and generalization capabilities.
∙ We demonstrate the robust predictive performance of HCMKT 
through experiments on four benchmark datasets. Moreover, the ef-
fectiveness of each module is validated via ablation studies.

2.1.  Knowledge tracing

due to their powerful learning and expressive abilities. Early deep learn-

data-driven memory matrices to model the evolutionary state of mas-

the progress of KT, they were limited to modeling shallow features in 
sequential data. Later, to eﬀectively model exercise sequences and cap-
ture long-term dependencies, researchers explored further KT models 
(Choi et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2020; Pandey & Karypis, 2019) based 
on attentional mechanisms. SAKT (Pandey & Karypis, 2019) introduced 
the Transformer ﬁrstly to learn attention matrices containing the rel-

2.2.  Contrastive learning
Contrastive learning (Su et al., 2024) constructs positive and neg-
ative instances from the data itself, by diﬀerentiating the distance be-
tween positive and negative instances in the feature space to learn high 
quality representations. Its powerful ability to extract potential features 
from unlabeled data has led to signiﬁcant results in various ﬁelds such 
as recommender systems (Yu et al., 2024). Recently, CL has gained high 
attention in the KT ﬁeld. CL4KT (Lee et al., 2022) imposed semantic re-
lations on student interaction history through contrastive learning. DT-
transformer (Yin et al., 2023) improved the stability of tracking results 
by mitigating information bias in the knowledge state modeling process. 
Bi-CLKT (Song et al., 2022) leveraged graph-level contrastive learning 
of question-level subgraphs and skill-level subgraphs to improve model 
performance. Although prior methods employ CL to extract semantic 
and structural information from learning records, they remain limited 
in capturing multi-granularity features in HIN. Therefore, the HCMKT 
constructs a novel contrastive view based on coarse and ﬁne-grained 
views, extracting macro-structural information and micro-semantic fea-
tures, improving model performance.
2.3.  Knowledge distillation
Knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Ma et al., 2025) is a DL 
technique that eﬀectively improves the performance and robustness of 
models through teacher-student network. It typically pre-trains a pow-
erful cumbersome network (teacher), which then transfers its learned 
knowledge to another compact network (student) with simple struc-
ture. The training quality of cumbersome network greatly aﬀects the 
model performance, and its two-stage training strategy often incurs sig-
niﬁcant computational overhead and time cost. To alleviate this draw-
back, online knowledge distillation (Chen et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2024; 
Wu & Gong, 2021; Zhang et al., 2018) directly trains models by extract-
ing knowledge from multi-branch networks. In this paper, we integrate 
coarse and ﬁne-grained modeling into the teacher network for collabo-
rative feature modeling, enhancing its representational power and gen-
eralization.

5.2.  Baselines
We evaluate model performance by comparing HCMKT to the fol-
lowing state-of-the-art methods:
∙ DKT (Piech et al., 2015) spearheaded the introduction of deep learn-
ing techniques in KT tasks, which are based on RNN and LSTM to 
model learning records for prediction of future student performance.
∙ DKVMN (Zhang et al., 2017a) used a static key memory matrix and 
a dynamic value memory matrix to track the evolution of student’s 
knowledge state.
∙ DKT+ (Yeung & Yeung, 2018) improved and optimized the DKT by 
introducing regularization terms in the loss fuction.
∙ SAKT (Pandey & Karypis, 2019) introduced the transformer ﬁrstly to 
learn attention matrices containing the relative weights of questions.
∙ AKT (Ghosh et al., 2020) further explored attentional mechanisms to 
develop monotonic attention, integrating it with psychological meth-
ods aligned with student’s learning processes.
∙ GKT (Nakagawa et al., 2019) introduced graph into KT for the ﬁrst 
time to mine information about structural relationships between 
skills.
∙ FGKT (Mao et al., 2023) modeled knowledge state from a ﬁne-
grained perspective by considering the relationship between skills 
and questions as well as questions and historical interactions.
∙ CL4KT (Lee et al., 2022) captured complex knowledge states by stim-
ulating semantic relationships in historical learning records through 
contrastive learning.
∙ DisKT (Zhou et al., 2025) eliminated the inﬂuence of confounder on 
the modeling of knowledge state by modeling student’s familiar and 
unfamiliar abilities.
∙ FCKT is a variant of our method that adds the question represen-
tations from the coarse and the ﬁne-grained views, and then feeds 
directly into the network for prediction.

models such as DKT and DKT+, memory-based models like DKVMN,

5.3.  Experimental settings
In order to ensure the conﬁdence level of the experiment, we use 
Area Under the Curve (AUC), Accuracy (ACC) and Root Mean Square 
Error (RMSE) as the performance evaluation metrics of all experiments. 
We split all the datasets in the ratio of 6:2:2, where 60 % is used for 
training and the rest for validation and testing respectively. We set the 
input sequence length of YMath to 57 while the rest of the datasets 
are ﬁxed to 200, and set the embedding dimensions (𝑑) of the question 
and the associated attribute factors to 256. Regarding feature masking 
and edge masking, the masking rate of ASSISTments2009 is set to {0.5, 
0.6} while the remaining datasets are set to {0.3, 0.2}. In addition, we 
set the number of clusters 𝑂 for ASSISTments2009, ASSISTments2017, 
Junyi and YMath to {100, 500}, {100, 300}, {8, 39} and {5, 10}. 𝜌𝑖𝑗
is determined by the number of times the anchor 𝑛𝑖 and the sample 𝑛𝑗
are located in the diﬀerent clusters, and the temperature parameter 𝜇
is ﬁxed to 0.5. We set the learning rate to 0.001, dropout to 0.2 and 
trained the model using the Adam optimizer.
5.4.  Performance analysis
We compared HCMKT with nine public methods and a variant of 
HCMKT to evaluate their performance. The results from the compari-
son experiments are presented in Table 4, with bold results indicating 
the best and underlined results indicating suboptimal. Table 4 shows 
that HCMKT outperformed other models across multiple evaluation met-
rics, demonstrating its eﬀectiveness in modeling student’s knowledge 
state and predicting future performance. HCMKT achieved the high-
est AUC score on the Junyi but ranked second to DisKT on the AS-
SISTments2017. This indicates that HCMKT enhances performance by 
leveraging multi-granularity features from learning records, though fur-
ther improvements in feature extraction are needed for data with vary-
ing distribution characteristics. HCMKT outperforms SAKT and AKT, 
which eﬀectively validates the importance of the question and its as-
sociated attribute factors for state modeling. The method based on 
the attention mechanism outperforms DKT, which only considers sin-
gle interaction information, in most of the results. GKT by construct-
ing skill or question relationship graphs is lower than HCMKT in per-
formance prediction, indicating that the coarse and ﬁne-grained views 
based on meta-path constructs eﬀectively capture the rich feature in-
formation contained in the heterogeneous relationships of the ques-
tions. This result is further validated by the variant FCKT, which out-
performs the rest of the comparison methods on ASSISTments2019 
and demonstrates strong competitiveness on other datasets. CL4KT is 
also weaker than HCMKT in capturing the state of student’s knowl-
edge using contrastive learning, suggesting that the negative sample
selection mechanism and contrastive learning employed in our method 
eﬀectively assist the network to learn high-quality question representa-
tions for tracking state evolution. Meanwhile, the online knowledge dis-
tillation adopted in HCMKT eﬀectively enhances the model’s modeling, 
prediction, and generalization abilities compared to the variant FCKT. 
To evaluate the reliability of experimental results, we assessed 5 meth-
ods on the ASSISTments2017 dataset, computing 95 % conﬁdence in-
tervals for AUC and ACC, along with p-values for statistical signiﬁcance 
relative to HCMKT. As shown in Fig. 4, DisKT, FCKT, and CL4KT demon-
strated signiﬁcant diﬀerences in AUC and ACC compared to HCMKT, 
whereas AKT exhibited no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence. These re-
sults consistent with Table 4, supporting the reliability of the observed 
performance diﬀerences and providing a solid foundation for method 
evaluation.

5.6.  Data sensing and parameter analysis
In order to explore the performance of HCMKT with diﬀerent ratios 
of training data, we take 20 %, 40 % and 60 % of the dataset to train the 
model, respectively. As shown in Fig. 5, HCMKT outperforms most of the 
baseline methods in the performance prediction for all four datasets. In 
addition, the variant FCKT also shows great competitiveness. This result 
eﬀectively demonstrates the model’s strong ability to capture higher-
order structural relationships in question heterogeneous graphs, as well 
as modeling implicit features of sequences. We found that DKVMN out-
performs our method when 20 % and 40 % training ratios are used in 
YMath. We attribute this to the fact that the memory structure employed 
by DKVMN eﬀectively models knowledge state changes when the data 
structure is simpler. However, when dealing with data having complex 
structural relationships, our model still outperforms DKVMN.
The embedding dimension 𝑑 aﬀects the models expressiveness. 
We investigated the impact of 𝑑 on the ASSISTments2017 and Junyi 
datasets. As shown in Fig. 6, model performance is optimal at the 𝑑 is 
set to 256. A smaller 𝑑 leads to insuﬃcient feature representation, while 
a larger 𝑑 may cause overﬁtting. We also explored the eﬀect of the tem-
perature parameter 𝜇 in Eq. (19), with results shown in Fig. 7. 𝜇 scales 
the input to adjust the activation function’s nonlinearity. Smaller 𝜇 val-
ues enhance nonlinearity, while larger values reduce it. In the HCMKT 
experiment, we set 𝜇 is to 0.5 to balance nonlinearity and model sta-
bility. Furthermore, we set 𝜆 to diﬀerent values for training to ﬁnd the 
optimal solution, and the results are shown in Fig. 8. After extensive 
experiments, we ﬁxed the values of 𝜆 under ASSISTments2009, ASSIST-
ments2017, Junyi and YMath to be 0.4, 0.001, 0.01 and 0.01. We found 
that when the number of questions is suﬃciently large, a relatively large 
value of 𝜆 better assists the network learning the question representa-
tions.
5.7.  Component analysis
In the state modeling module, HCMKT uses LSTM for knowledge 
state modeling. To explore the performance of RNN, GRU, and LSTM 
in encoding state information, we conducted component analysis ex-
periments, as shown in Table 6. The results show that LSTM outper-
forms RNN and GRU. Firstly, LSTM mitigates the gradient vanishing 
problem of traditional RNN through its sophisticated gating mechanism. 
Secondly, it exhibits stronger representation ability than GRU, despite 
requiring more parameters. Therefore, LSTM’s advanced gating struc-
ture makes it better suited for HCMKT modeling.

5.9.  KT application discussion
KT has a wide range of practical application value in education. First, 
KT can eﬃciently process massive education data, providing educated 
with accurate assessments of knowledge state. Secondly, online educa-
tion platforms can leverage KT to recommend personalized learning re-
sources based on students’ knowledge state, addressing the personal-
ization limitations of traditional statistical methods. Finally, in actual 
education scenarios, educators can continuously monitor the changes in 
student’s knowledge state through KT, dynamically optimize curriculum 
design, and foster eﬀective alignment between teaching and learning.
5.10.  Weakness discussion
Extensive experiments have conﬁrmed that HCMKT eﬀectively eval-
uates students’ knowledge state by mining high-order feature informa-
tion from HIN. However, our method still has some limitations. On the

6.  Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a novel knowledge tracing method HCMKT. 
It constructs a coarse view to represent meta-path connection relation-
ships between questions and a ﬁne-grained view to describe the seman-
tic details of connections. To capture macro-level structural information 
and micro-level semantic features between questions, we construct con-
trastive learning views by introducing noise and an elaborated negative 
sample selection mechanism. We then integrate question and answer 
representations to model student’s knowledge state for predicting fu-
ture performance. To enhance the model’s ability to model, predict, and 
generalize, we combine contrastive learning with online knowledge dis-
tillation. Extensive experiments demonstrate HCMKT’s eﬀectiveness and 
superior performance in handling complex data. In the future, we plan 
to further explore higher-order feature information in students’ learning 
records to accurately track the evolution of their knowledge state.
