A B S T R A C T
Knowledge tracing (KT) is a crucial task in online education that traces students’ evolving cognition changes
over time. However, it is a challenging task due to the heterogeneity of knowledge and incomplete cognition
evolution sequences. This paper proposes KT-Deeper, a long-term Knowledge Tracing framework based on
Dynamic reinforced heterogeneous graph contrastive networks, to predict students’ cognitive states on specific
skills. Particularly, KT-Deeper initially employs temporal heterogeneous graphs to model the interconnections
between different types of knowledge entities ( e.g., students, exercises, and skills). Subsequently, KT-Deeper
formalizes knowledge tracing as a dynamic link prediction task on the temporal heterogeneous graph sequence
and proposes a reinforced graph generation approach to refine the incomplete graph sequence for supporting
long-term knowledge tracing. KT-Deeper further presents a self-supervised heterogeneous graph embedding
method to extract the structural features of knowledge evolution. Finally, KT-Deeper leverages recurrent neural
networks to learn the temporal features of students’ cognitive evolution and predict whether a student will
master a specific skill. Experimental results confirm that KT-Deeper exhibits superior performance compared
to existing cutting-edge techniques, showcasing its promising accuracy and robustness in incomplete long-term
knowledge tracing tasks.
1. Introduction
Teaching plays a central role in the dissemination of knowledge,
with teachers able to monitor and assess the learning progress of their
students, allowing them to adapt their teaching pace, materials, and
methodology to optimize each student’s knowledge gain [ 1]. With
the rapid advancement of the Internet, students have increasingly
accessible opportunities to pursue knowledge through various online
platforms such as MOOC, Coursera, and Udacity. Although online
education has made remarkable progress in recent decades, it still faces
numerous challenges, the most serious of which is how to effectively
trace a student’s cognitive progress based on their question–answer in-
teraction and exercise quality [2], namely knowledge tracing (KT) task
in this paper. Generally, the objective of knowledge tracing is to trace
and assess students’ cognition changes in specific skills by analyzing
their performance in exercise over some time, it is of great significance
for providing individualized guidance and adaptive learning to students
on online tutoring platforms.
Knowledge tracing has been extensively studied for several decades,
leading to the development of numerous knowledge tracing approaches

• This paper proposes KT-Deeper, a long-term knowledge trac-

effectively captured by KT-Deeper, demonstrating promising ef-
fectiveness and robustness in long-term knowledge tracing with
incomplete evolutionary sequences.

2. Related work
2.1. Knowledge tracing
Machine Learning-Based Knowledge Tracing. As a pioneering
work of knowledge tracing, Albert et al. proposed BKT [4], which
used Bayesian networks and hidden Markov models to estimate a
student’s cognition level on a specific skill. Similarly, Michael et al. [19]
developed an individualized Bayesian knowledge tracing model by
incorporating more student features into BKT to boost the prediction
performance on unseen students. Afterward, some variants based on
BKT have emerged, such as introducing students’ learning ability [20],
the effect of exercise difficulty [21], and prior knowledge and relations
among skills [22]. Meanwhile, the logistic model [23] is developed
for knowledge tracing, which utilizes logistic functions to measure
the probability that a student correctly answering exercises. Jill et al.
proposed KTM [24], an extensible knowledge tracing model based on
factorization machines, to quickly trace students’ knowledge evolution
patterns. Although these initial approaches create the breakthrough
in knowledge tracing, they over-rely on narrow linear models with
limited capability in learning students’ cognition evolution [5], which
inevitably results in inefficient performance of knowledge tracing.
Deep Learning-Based Knowledge Tracing. With the significant
surge in deep learning, various types of neural networks have been
cultivated to model students’ cognition patterns for knowledge trac-
ing [3]. Jill et al. proposed DKT [5], the path-breaking knowledge
tracing framework using recurrent neural networks (RNNs). DKT as-
sumed that students have the same learning ability and utilized hidden
neural units to capture students’ cognitive state over time and predicted
their capability to master specific skills. To relax their assumption, Jill
et al. further presented Deep-DKT [25], a deeper DKT incorporated
dynamic student classification based on their different learning abili-
ties. Different from DKT and Deep-DKT, dynamic key–value memory
networks (DKVMN [26]) and sequential key–value memory networks
(STVMN [6]) were proposed to incorporate the strengths from recurrent
neural networks and memory networks for capturing dynamic knowl-
edge evolution patterns. As attention mechanism [27] has achieved
remarkable success in deep learning, it has been introduced into the
knowledge tracing field. Particularly, Ghosh et al. proposed a context-
aware attention knowledge tracing method (AKT [7]), using monotonic
attention to obtain better prediction results and improve interpretabil-
ity. Afterward, Pandey et al. proposed SAKT [8], a knowledge trac-
ing framework based on a self-attention mechanism, which utilized
self-attention to learn knowledge concepts from students’ historical
interactions to address data sparsity in knowledge tracing. Further-
more, several studies incorporated deep sequence models to boost the
performance of knowledge tracing, including the text content of exer-
cises [28,29], temporal dependence between skills [30], and students’
individual learning ability and knowledge acquisition behaviors [31].
These methods can effectively obtain isolated deep features, but the
interdependent relationships among knowledge objects ( e.g., students,
exercises, skills) are ignored, making their performance limited.
Graph-Based Knowledge Tracing. Recently, with the impressive
achievements of graph neural networks (GNNs) in mining interconnect
data, some graph-based knowledge tracing approaches have been pro-
posed. More specifically, Nakagawa et al. presented GKT [13], a knowl-
edge tracing framework based on graph neural networks, which mod-
eled the knowledge concepts with a graph and transformed knowledge
tracing as temporal node classification task on the graph. Tong et al.
presented a structure-based knowledge tracing model named SKT [14],
which simultaneously investigated temporal features from exercise se-
quence and spatial features from graph structures to evaluate students’
learning abilities. To effectively grasp the long-term dependency be-
tween students’ exercises, GIKT [15] is presented, which utilizes a
message propagation mechanism and historical exercise sequences to
trace the long-term knowledge states of students. Liu et al. devel-
oped PEBG [16], a pre-training knowledge tracing framework, which
pre-trained question embeddings and explored four side information
including exercise-skill connection, exercise similarities, skill similar-
ities, and difficulty constraint to improve gains of knowledge tracing.
Cui et al. presented a dual graph ensemble learning framework called
DGEKT [17], to explore the heterogeneous exercise-concept relation-
ships and predict students’ next cognition state by hypergraph learn-
ing. Despite these methods having achieved impressive progress, the
strengths of incorporating heterogeneous knowledge objects ( e.g., stu-
dents, exercises, skills) and dynamic cognition evolution analysis are
under-explored. Moreover, existing works do not notice the problem
of data incompleteness in long-term knowledge tracing, which severely
degrades the prediction performance. By contrast, we construct tem-
poral dynamic heterogeneous graphs to capture the interconnectedness
between students, exercises, and skills, and propose a dynamic graph
generation method to address data incompleteness, effectively boosting
the performance in long-term knowledge tracing.
2.2. Dynamic graph generation
Graph generation is a crucial problem in the field of graph data
mining, which learns the underlying distributions of given graphs and
generates novel ones. The sequential graph generation method repre-
sents a dominant paradigm for this task, treating graph generation as a
sequential decision-making process to generate nodes and edges. More
specifically, the MPNN [32] is proposed, a graph generation method
based on hierarchical recurrent neural networks (RNN), wherein the
outer RNN generates the nodes and the inner RNN infers the associated
edges between nodes. You et al. proposed GraphRNN [18], a deep au-
toregressive model that approximates the distribution of graphs without
assuming their underlying structure, which learns to generate graphs by
training on prior graphs and decomposes the graph generation process
into a sequential formation of node and edges. After, Liu et al. enhanced
the diversity of GraphRNN in generating graphs by introducing novel
evaluation functions [33]. Bacciu et al. designed a sequential process-
based graph generation method relying on a node ordering procedure,
it utilized two RNNs to learn and predict the potential edges between
nodes [34]. Sequential generation exhibits the capability to make local
decisions, yet it encounters challenges in preserving long-term depen-
dencies. To mitigate the limitations, reinforcement learning has been
incorporated into graph generation. Particularly, GAM [35] focused
on processing noisy graph-structured data and employed the Partially
Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) for training models,
enabling selective information extraction from the graph to generate
novel topological structures. Moreover, NetRL [36] further enhances
graph generation capability by utilizing MDP to identify erroneous links
and predict absent links. Sun et al. presented Surge [37], a framework
for hierarchical subgraph-level selection and generating more discrim-
inative subgraph representations by utilizing MDP to control sampling
strategies.
Moreover, MDP-based graph generation (or representation) ap-
proaches have been used in various fields. Particularly, Dou et al.
proposed the CARE-GNN model for detecting camouflaged fraudsters,
which leverages MDP to select top-k similarity nodes and effectively
filter out the camouflaged nodes in information aggregation [38]. To
unveil subtle vulnerabilities in graph-based rumor detection models,
Lyu et al. [39] employed MDP to simulate attack behaviors of cam-
ouflage rumors for enhancing the robustness of their model. IG-RL
model [40] is implemented for massive-scale traffic signal control,
wherein an MDP mechanism is employed to generate vehicle trajec-
tories on the road network and acquire a transferable adaptive policy
for traffic signal control. Fang et al. proposed QADD model for drug
design, which adopts a multi-objective MDP to generate molecules with
multiple desired properties [41]. In the field of traffic control, Nie and
Xing et al. integrated reinforcement learning algorithms with MDP to
formulate the optimal strategy for traffic management based on the
predicted traffic flow [42,43].

4. Methodology
We propose KT-Deeper to demystify students’ cognitive patterns
in knowledge acquisition and implement knowledge tracing for long-
term sequences with incomplete data. Fig. 3 depicts the framework
of KT-Deeper, comprising three components: (a) Dynamic graph gen-
eration module utilizes reinforcement learning to automatically gen-
erate short-term graph sequence to complete the missing data during
time [𝑡 + 1,𝑡 + 𝑛 − 1] . (b) Heterogeneous graph embedding mod-
ule learns the high-level representations of different graph snapshots
 = { 1, 2, …, 𝑡, …, 𝑡+𝑛−1} by maximizing the global–local mutual
information of each graph to obtain temporal embedding sequence
𝐻 = { ℎ1,ℎ 2, …,ℎ𝑡, …,ℎ𝑡+𝑛−1}, which carries the evolutionary pat-
terns of knowledge cognition. (c) The learned temporal graph em-
bedding sequences are fed into LSTM networks to capture the spa-
tiotemporal evolution patterns based on historical graph sequences  =
{1, 2, …, 𝑡, …, 𝑡+𝑛−1} and infer the newly emerged links in 𝑡+𝑛 to
predict whether students will master specific skills at time𝑡 +𝑛. Table 1

5.2. Overall experiment analysis
To verify the efficacy of our proposed KT-Deeper in long-term
knowledge tracing, we conduct a comparative analysis against state-
of-the-art methods and record their 5-fold cross-validation results in-
cluding AUROC and Recall, as presented in Table 3. The experimental
outcomes demonstrate that our proposed KT-Deeper surpasses the con-
ventional approaches, and its performance gains can be ascribed to the
subsequent characteristics.
(1) It is widely acknowledged that knowledge objects (e.g., students,
exercises, skills) are not isolated but rather interconnected [52]. How-
ever, traditional deep learning-based methods (e.g., DKT, DKVMN, AKT,
and SAKT) typically focus on extracting the temporal features from
exercises to predict students’ knowledge states. Despite these methods
can capture shallow cognition evolutionary patterns based on isolated
exercise sequences, the intricate ones behind interdependent relation-
ships among heterogeneous knowledge objects cannot be captured,
which inevitably undermines the performances of knowledge tracing.
In contrast, our proposed KT-Deeper leverages temporal heterogeneous
graphs to model interactive relationships among students, exercises,
and skills, which can effectively uncover their inherent interconnect-
edness and capture the intricate temporal evolutionary patterns to
improve prediction performance. As a result, compared with tradi-
tional deep learning-based approaches, KT-Deeper achieves more than
11.54%, 12.16%, 9.56%, 10.93%, and 12.02% performance gains in
terms of AUROC on ASSIST09, ASSIST17, EdNet, SDNU, and SVTU
datasets, respectively.
(2) Static graph-based methods such as Line, Esim, HAN, VGAE, and
ARVGE only concentrate on students’ cognition states at specific time
𝑡 and employ it to predict students’ next cognitive state at time 𝑡 + 1.
Different from static graph-based approaches, our proposed KT-Deeper
focuses on students’ long-term historical cognitive process. KT-Deeper
utilizes temporal heterogeneous graphs to model students’ dynamic
cognition evolution information over time, and the temporal hetero-
geneous graph sequence  = { 1, 2, …, 𝑡} contains the historical
cognition evolution information of each student. Meanwhile, LSTM
as an efficient RNN framework for capturing long-term dependency
can effectively learn students’ cognition evolution patterns using LSTM
hidden units to aggregate students’ historical evolution information.
Obviously, this historical evolution information enhances the perfor-
mance of long-term knowledge tracing. Thus, the proposed KT-Deeper
obtains more than 12.07%, 12.40%, 10.81%, 8.98%, and 9.89% im-
provement against static graph-based methods in terms of AUROC on
ASSIST09, ASSIST17, EdNet, SDNU, and SVTU, respectively.
(3) Current dynamic graph-based models ( e.g., GKT, SKT, GIKT,
PEBG, DGEKT) construct a temporal graph sequence  = {1, 2, …, 𝑡}
to capture the evolving patterns of knowledge cognition and predict
students’ cognition state at time 𝑡 + 1 . These methods assume that
students’ cognition evolutionary data is complete. However, in real-
world knowledge tracing scenarios, the temporal data is difficult to
collect in real-time or even missing, which hurts the performance of
long-term knowledge tracing. To address the limitation, KT-Deeper
proposes a dynamic graph generation method to refine the incomplete
data sequence during time [𝑡+1,𝑡 +𝑛−1], which effectively supports the
long-term knowledge tracing at 𝑡 +𝑛. By utilizing the dynamic graph
generation, KT-Deeper boosts the AUROC performances with 4.48%,
3.86%, 5.06%, 6.03%, and 7.72% gains against dynamic graph-based
approaches on ASSIST09, ASSIST17, EdNet, SDNU, and SVTU, respec-
tively. Overall, the experimental results demonstrate that our proposed
KT-Deeper model exhibits promising performance in knowledge tracing
across diverse educational backgrounds and various exercise types.

6.2. Computational complexity analysis
The computational complexity of KT-Deeper is mainly composed
of two parts: 𝑂 = 𝑇 + 𝑄, where 𝑇 denotes the time complexity in-
volved in graph generation in reinforcement learning, and𝑄 represents
the computational complexity associated with graph embedding. More
specifically, considering the heterogeneous graph  = ( , ), a meta-
path set 𝑃 = { 𝑝1, …,𝑝𝑚, …,𝑝𝑀 }, and the Markov decision process
𝐵 = ( 𝐴,𝑆,𝑅,𝑇 ). The time complexity of graph generation is 𝑃 =
𝑂(|𝑆|3 + |𝑆|2 ⋅ |𝐴|), where 𝑆 denotes the state space, while 𝐴 signi-
fies its corresponding action space. The computational complexity of
heterogeneous graph embedding is represented by 𝑄 =𝑂(|𝜉|𝑀𝑁𝑁𝐷 ),
where 𝑁 and 𝑀 indicate the number of nodes and metapaths in ,
𝐷 is the feature dimension of nodes, and ‖𝜉‖ represents the number of
edges across all metapaths. Thus, the overall computational complexity
of KT-Deeper can be denoted 𝑂 = |𝑆|3 +|𝑆|2⋅|𝐴| +|𝜉|𝑀𝑁𝑁𝐷 .
Consequently, we compare the runtime cost between KT-Deeper and
the graph-based approaches on 21,765 edges involving 2856 nodes. To
ensure fairness, all experiments are conducted on an Intel(R) Core(TM)
i7-6700 CPU@3.40 GHz processor with 16 cores, 128 GB RAM, and 2×
NVIDIA Tesla K80 GPUs. The codes are implemented in Python 3.7 and
PyTorch framework on the Ubuntu 16.0.4 operating system. As shown
in Fig. 8, the runtime of KT-Deeper is longer than that of conventional
methods. By analyzing program timing, we have identified that the
extra time spent by KT-deeper primarily pertains to graph genera-
tion in reinforcement learning. Integrating reinforcement learning into
KT-Deeper may lead to increased computational requirements, but it
enhances the precision of knowledge tracing. It is justifiable to trade
off a small amount of time consumption for improved accuracy in
non-real-time knowledge tracing tasks.
6.3. Interpretability analysis
The proposed KT-Deeper surpasses existing deep learning-based
knowledge tracing models in terms of interpretability, manifesting itself
in two distinct aspects. Firstly, in contrast to graph methods based
on random walking, KT-Deeper incorporates metapaths (see Fig. 2)
that have real-world applicability for exploring interactive relationships
among students, exercises, and skills. Compared to random walking,
metapaths capture real-world semantic relationships ( e.g., the meta-
path 𝑝1 in Fig. 2 depicts the semantic template that two students
correctly answer exercises), thereby it offers enhanced interpretability
than random walking mechanism lacking such real-world semantic
significance.
Secondly, the KT-Deeper leverages temporal heterogeneous graphs
to depict the sequential correlation among knowledge, while incorpo-
rating weight learning and principles of students’ knowledge cognition
to enhance its interpretability. Specifically, we showcase the inter-
pretability of the KT-Deeper model through weight visualization and
analysis of knowledge causality. As illustrated in Fig. 9, consider-
ing skill node 0 ( i.e., triple integral), it is represented with nodes
1∼4. According to the principles of advanced mathematics, it is well-
established that when aggregating information for embedding node
0 (i.e., triple integral), the definite integral (i.e., node 2) and double inte-
gral (i.e., node 4) are more effective in characterizing the triple integral
than function limit (i.e., node 1) and differential coefficient (i.e., node3).
Evidently, within the KT-Deeper framework, higher weights are as-
signed to the definite integral (0.355) and double integral (0.432),
respectively. Meanwhile, we further analyze the predicted results from
the perspective of knowledge causality. We found the KT-Deeper pre-
dicted that 94.68% of students who mastered the double integral
would also be capable of learning triple integral. Conversely, the model
predicted that only 1.02% of students who did not mastered the double
integral would achieve proficiency in triple integral. These prediction
results align with the reality of knowledge causality, which brings
promising interpretability to a certain extent.
7. Conclusion
To improve the performance gains of long-term knowledge tracing,
we propose a robust knowledge tracing model based on dynamic rein-
forced heterogeneous graph contrastive networks, namely KT-Deeper.
KT-Deeper effectively addresses the challenge of long-term knowledge
tracing with incomplete data. Firstly, to demystify the evolving rela-
tionships among different types of knowledge objects ( e.g., students,
exercises, and skills), we leverage temporal heterogeneous graphs to
model heterogeneous objects and design real-world metapaths to ex-
plore their interactive relationships. Secondly, we devise a dynamic
graph generation approach based on reinforcement learning to renovate
the incomplete graph sequence during [𝑡 + 1,𝑡 +𝑛 − 1] for obtaining the
completed graph sequence  = {1, 2, …, 𝑡, …, 𝑡+𝑛−1}, which is com-
petent for the long-term knowledge tracing at time step 𝑡 +𝑛. Thirdly,
we propose a self-supervised heterogeneous graph embedding method
to learn the structure features of𝑡 at different time steps and obtain the
graph representation sequence 𝐻 = {ℎ1,ℎ 2, …,ℎ𝑡, …,ℎ𝑡+𝑛−1}. Finally,
the graph representation sequence is fed into LSTM networks to capture
students’ cognition evolutionary trends and predict whether a student
will master a specific skill. Our proposed KT-Deeper demonstrates
superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in
long-term knowledge tracing with incomplete temporal data.
Despite KT-Deeper achieving promising performance in knowledge
tracing, it still has certain limitations that need to be addressed in
the future. Firstly, the generalization of the model requires additional
verification across diverse educational scenarios encompassing differ-
ent educational contexts, exercise types, and student demographics.
In the further, we intend to collect a more comprehensive set of
experiment data to refine our model and enhance its performance in
knowledge tracing. Moreover, in the process of real-world knowledge
acquisition, students frequently encounter memory decay and forget a
portion of what they have learned. Incorporating students’ knowledge
forgetting law into our model to accurately capture their cognitive
processes remains an unresolved challenge. In future research, we aim
to employ multi-agent reinforcement learning techniques to simulate
students’ knowledge decay behavior, thereby enhancing the accuracy
and generalization capabilities of KT-Deeper.
