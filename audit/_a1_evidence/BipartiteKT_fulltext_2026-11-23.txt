Abstract. The use of online educational platforms has become an
increasingly popular way for people to acquire knowledge from the Inter-
net. Knowledge tracking tasks are deﬁned as predicting learners’ future
performance based on their historical interaction data. Many researchers
employ the bipartite graph representation of the relationship between
two diﬀerent types of nodes, exercises and knowledge concepts, to make
better predictions. However, the implicit associations between nodes of
the same type in this graph cannot be well captured, which limits the
model’s ability to extract deep relationships and semantic information,
resulting in poor generalisation ability. To address this limitation, we
propose a bipartite graph-based contrastive learning method with dual-
view data augmentation, which captures implicit associations between
nodes of the same type while improving the representation of relation-
ships between exercises and concepts. In addition, the application of
deep learning methods in knowledge tracing often suﬀers from poor
interpretability of prediction results. To address this issue, this paper
incorporates the mastery scores of exercises and knowledge concepts into
the predictions to improve the interpretability of the model. Experimen-
tal results show that our model outperforms related models on several
datasets.

1 Introduction
The development of Knowledge Tracing (KT), an important ﬁeld in educational
technology, aims to analyse learners’ behaviour and performance data to assess
their knowledge acquisition in real time and predict future learning outcomes.
With the rapid advancement of information technology and data science, knowl-
edge tracing techniques have also been constantly evolving and innovating. While

traditional models such as Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) [ 2] have laid
the groundwork by using probabilistic methods to assess student mastery of
knowledge items, they often struggle with the complexity of larger data sets
and diverse learning behaviours. Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) [ 14] repre-

works (RNNs) to handle more complex data and learning patterns, signiﬁcantly
improving prediction accuracy.
Regarding the inherent data sparsity problem in knowledge tracing, schol-
ars have attempted to address this issue by exploiting the relationship graphs
between exercises and knowledge concepts, but existing methods still face signif-
icant challenges. This paper focuses on two prominent issues: (1) The intrinsic
associations between nodes of the same type in this bipartite graph are not eﬀec-
tively captured, which limits the model’s ability to extract deep connections and
semantic information. In the real world, learning concepts in one domain can
help you understand similar concepts in another domain. There are also simi-
lar solutions between diﬀerent exercises. These connections are often overlooked
in traditional models, weakening the models’ ability to generalise across diﬀer-
ent educational settings. (2) Although existing deep learning models have good
predictive capabilities, due to tens of thousands of parameters, these models
often lack ﬁne-grained interpretability and cannot analyse their inference pro-
cess based on the prediction results. As a result, online learning platforms are
unable to more comprehensively analyse learners’ learning situations based on
KT tasks to make recommendations for further learning.
To address these critical gaps, our research proposes innovative solutions: (1)
introduces a novel bipartite graph-based contrastive learning approach to knowl-
edge tracing, which can capture the implicit associations within the exercise set
and within the knowledge concept set, respectively, and enhance the depth of
associations between exercises and concepts, thereby enriching the representation
of the model and increasing its adaptability to diverse learning environments;
(2) addresses the common problem of low interpretability associated with deep
learning models in KT, we integrate the mastery scores of exercises and knowl-
edge concepts into our predictions, allowing us to explain the result from two
perspectives: understanding of the exercise and mastery of the knowledge con-
cepts. This enhancement signiﬁcantly increases the interpretability of our model,
providing educators with a more transparent and actionable understanding of
predicted student outcomes.
In summary, we are bridging the gap between advanced predictive accuracy
and the essential need for interpretability in educational applications, with the
aim of fostering a more informed and adaptive educational landscape, tailored
to the diverse and evolving needs of learners.
2 Related Work
The rapid evolution of deep learning has inspired a multitude of approaches
to address the intricacies of Knowledge Tracing, each seeking to enhance the
accuracy and depth of learner analysis within intelligent tutoring systems.

Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) [14] represents a shift in knowledge tracing
research into the era of deep learning, using recurrent neural networks (RNN)
to predict learner performance. Contrastive Learning for Knowledge Tracing
(CL4FT) [ 24] and DTransformer [ 22] introduce the framework of sequence-
based contrastive learning, which aims to produce robust embeddings of learner
interactions by distinguishing between positive and negative samples, thereby
strengthening the accuracy and resilience of knowledge state predictions. FGKT
[9] obtains exercise representations based on knowledge concepts and diﬀer-
ences, and uses an attention mechanism to capture the relevance between assess-
ment exercises and historical interactions to assess individual prior knowledge.
GKT [10], GIKT [ 20]a n dD B G C N[6] integrate graph structures to capture
higher order relationships and dependencies between knowledge. KSGAN [ 8]
fully exploits the knowledge structure between concepts and exercises to obtain
eﬀective exercise representations and reduce the sparsity of educational data.
DEGKT [3] uses hypergraph modeling and directed graph modeling to establish
the dual graph structure of student learning interaction to capture the hetero-
geneous practice of concept association and interactive transformation. These
models exploit the relational dynamics of educational data, transforming the
relationships between exercises and concepts into graph representations to enrich
predictive capabilities. This graph-based strategy deepens the models’ under-
standing of complex educational interdependencies and provides a more nuanced
representation of knowledge states. However, the implicit associations between
nodes of the same type in this graph cannot be well captured, which limits the
model’s ability to extract deep-level associations and semantic information, and
the model’s generalization ability is weak.
Additionally, interpretability remains a challenge in KT, particularly with
sophisticated models. Deep-IRT [ 21] employs the Item Response Theory [ 5]
model to estimate the probability that a student will answer an item correctly
using estimated student ability and item diﬃculty, providing a psychological
interpretation of both students and items. Interpretable Knowledge Tracing
(IKT) [16] seeks to bridge this gap by incorporating three principal elements
into its prediction algorithm: mastery of individual concepts, exercise diﬃculty,
and learners’ response tendencies, thereby yielding more transparent insights
into the educational process. While IKT revisits the BKT framework for its pre-
dictions, it still faces limitations in handling large-scale data. QIKT [1] employs
an item response theory based prediction layer to produce interpretable pre-
diction results based on the utilization of deep learning models. However, the
experimental data from the corresponding model papers indicate that these KT
models that account for interpretability often result in a trade-oﬀ between pre-
dictive performance and interpretability.
To combine the strengths and mitigate the limitations of these predeces-
sors, our research proposes a novel bipartite graph-based contrastive learning
approach to knowledge tracing that enhances the representation of these inter-
actions and captures deep connections across exercises and concepts. In addition,

3 Methodology
3.1 Problem Deﬁnition
Knowledge tracing aims to capture learners’ knowledge state from their past
learning interactions and predict future exercises. Speciﬁcally, learners’ practice
interactions are formalised as a sequence of tuples {x1,x 2,...,x t}, where xt =
(et,r t) represents the result rt of a learner answering the exercise et at time
step t. Here, rt ∈{ 1,0} indicates whether the learner’s answer to the exercise is
correct or not. The goal of knowledge tracing is to predict whether the learner’s
answer to the exercise e
t+1 at the next time step will be correct, based on the
sequence of learner interactions from time step 1 to t. We use two metrics to
evaluate the performance of the model: AUC (area under the curve) and ACC
(accuracy). Higher values indicate a better predictive ability of the model.
3.2 Model Overview
The main structure of the model is shown in Fig. 1. We have divided it into
two main parts: the prediction part and the bipartite graph contrastive learning
part. The prediction part primarily employs a modiﬁed version of the GATs
(Graph Attention Layer) [18] which uses a bipartite graph of exercises, knowledge
concepts, and their relations as embeddings for training. This is combined with
the learner’s knowledge state of exercises and knowledge concepts to improve the
accuracy of predictions. The bipartite graph contrastive learning part includes
two perspectives: inter-class and intra-class. Finally, the total loss is obtained by
combining the learner’s exercise mastery state loss, knowledge concept mastery
state loss, prediction loss, and the bipartite graph contrastive learning total loss
with diﬀerent weights, which is then optimized.

3.5 Bipartite Graph Contrastive Learning
Inspired by SBGCL [24], we propose an unsupervised unsigned bipartite graph
contrastive learning model that learns deep associations and semantic informa-
tion between exercises and concepts and the implicit relationships between nodes
of the same type. Figure 1 shows the overall architecture. Bipartite graph con-
trastive learning mainly consists of two steps: (1) graph data augmentation; (2)
dual-view contrastive learning. Speciﬁcally, we ﬁrst create two diﬀerent views of
the original bipartite graph, namely the inter-class view and the intra-class view,
capturing the explicit relationships between knowledge concepts and exercises
r(e
i,c j) and the implicit relationships between their internal nodes r(ei,e j),
r(ci,c j). In each graph view, we use random graph augmentation to generate
augmented graphs, which are combined with the original graph to form positive
sample pairs and encoded as adjacency matrices. Finally, we calculate contrastive
loss for each view, maximizing the similarity between positive sample pairs to
improve the performance of the model.
Data Augmentation. Due to the discreteness of data annotations, most exer-
cises involve few knowledge concepts, and some concepts correspond to few exer-
cises, leading to sparse intra-class correlation matrices. To support model embed-
ding and graph contrastive learning, we augment graphs representing inter-class
and intra-class node relationships, increasing their density and enhancing model
generalization. Previous works [25] use random graph augmentations by perturb-
ing graph structures and attributes, aiming to learn representations that remain
robust to these perturbations [ 19]. Before training, we augment the exercise-
concept bipartite graph G
1, exercise correlation bipartite graph G2, and knowl-
edge concept correlation bipartite graphG3 by randomly adding or deleting edges

4.2 Baselines and Experimental Settings
We conducted a comparative analysis of our proposed model against several
established baseline models, which include:
– DKT [ 14] employs RNN to model learning process. In our experiments, we
develop DKT with LSTM network.
– DKVMN [ 23] utilizes a memory-augmented neural network to predict learn-
ers’ future performance.
–S A K T [12] employs a pure self-attention mechanism to make predictions for
KT.
– KSGAN [8] explores the structural relationships between exercises and knowl-
edge concepts to deepen the understanding of their interrelationships.
–C L 4 F T [24] incorporates contrastive learning to reﬁne feature representation,
improving the model’s ability to capture subtle nuances.
–Q I K T [1] provides ﬁne-grained, question-centric cognitive representations by
integrating knowledge acquisition and problem-solving modules.
Each model brings distinct methodologies and enhancements to the ﬁeld of
knowledge tracing, providing diﬀerent perspectives on how to best model and
understand student learning trajectories.
4.3 Implementation Details
The Area Under Curve (AUC) and Accuracy (ACC) are used as the metrics
to report the performance. In each experiment, an Adam optimizer is adopted
to minimize the loss value. The embedding dimension d, batch size, dropout
rate, learning rate and maximum length N are set to 64, 16, 0.3, 0.005 and 50,
respectively. Moreover, the candidate values for the hyperparametersλ
ca,λp and

Python with Pytorch on a Linux server.

4.7 Conclusion
This paper presents the Contrastive Learning Bipartite Graph (CLBG) model, a
novel approach designed to improve the prediction accuracy and interpretability
of Knowledge Tracing (KT) systems. By incorporating a bipartite graph con-
trastive learning module alongside an interpretability prediction module, CLBG
eﬀectively leverages the intricate relationships between exercises and knowledge
concepts, ensuring a deep and nuanced understanding of learner data.
Unlike traditional KT models, which may rely on additional external infor-
mation or simpler interactions, CLBG focuses solely on the intrinsic knowledge
structure embedded within educational data. This targeted approach allows for
more accurate predictions by directly modeling the complex interrelationships
inherent in the learning process. The model’s dual focus on contrastive learning
and interpretability ensures that it not only achieves high accuracy but also pro-
vides clear, actionable insights into the learning trajectories of students, which
are critical for practical educational applications. The eﬀectiveness of the CLBG
model has been rigorously tested against several baseline models on multiple
real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance in both AUC and ACC
metrics.
