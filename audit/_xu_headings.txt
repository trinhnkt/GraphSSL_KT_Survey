Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a fundamental task in intelligent education that tracks students’ knowledge
states through their historical interactions. Early deep learning-based KT mainly relies on sequential
models, which often find it more challenging to explicitly model the complex many-to-many relation-
ships between students and knowledge entities, such as exercises and knowledge components. Graph-
based Knowledge Tracing (GbKT) further leverages graph neural networks to explicitly represent these
structural relationships, creating new opportunities for learning-state monitoring and personalized inter-
vention. In this survey, we conduct an in-depth exploration of recent progress in GbKT research. First, we
introduce a two-dimensional taxonomy that aligns the primary modeling motivations with graph structural
forms, and we characterize them with unified message-passing equations. Second, focusing on applica-
tions, such as the discovery of knowledge structures, modeling with educational psychology theories and
interpretability, and personalized recommendation and path planning, we show how graph semantics en-
dow the models with new interpretability and intervention capabilities. Third, we systematically evaluate
17 public datasets, offering researchers guidance on dataset selection. Finally, we outline promising fu-
ture research directions for GbKT.
Keywords:knowledge tracing, graph-based knowledge tracing, graph neural networks, student model-
ing, educational data mining, personalized learning
1. INTRODUCTION
In AI-driven online learning environments, Knowledge Tracing (KT) (Corbett and Anderson,
1994) is a key task, aiming to accurately predict students’ performance on the next exercise on
the basis of their interaction records with previous exercises (Dowling and Hockemeyer, 2001;

Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) (Corbett and Anderson, 1994) is the foundational model
in the knowledge tracing field. BKT is based on a Hidden Markov Model and uses four prob-
ability parameters—initial mastery probability, learning probability, guess probability, and slip
probability—to independently model each knowledge component. It updates the mastery proba-
bility estimate via Bayesian inference after each student response and has been widely deployed
in intelligent tutoring systems such as Cognitive Tutor and MATHia. Notably, BKT remains an
active research direction, with recent advances in interpretability (Zhou et al., 2024), parameter
theory (Shchepakin et al., 2024), and fairness (Barrett et al., 2024).
Meanwhile, the rise of Deep Learning Knowledge Tracing (DLKT) has opened new di-
rections for KT research. Compared to BKT’s assumption of independent modeling for each
KC, DLKT can capture more complex cross-skill learning dynamics and nonlinear temporal
dependencies. DKT (Piech et al., 2015) pioneered the application of recurrent neural networks
to KT; Dynamic Key-Value Memory Network (DKVMN) (Zhang et al., 2017) introduced dy-
namic key-value memory networks to directly output mastery levels for each concept. Subse-
quently, attention-based models further advanced the field: Self-Attentive KT (SAKT) (Pandey
and Karypis, 2019) and context-aware Attentive KT (AKT) (Ghosh et al., 2020) introduced self-
attention mechanisms to model the relevance of historical interactions and forgetting behavior;
separated Self-Attentive Neural KT+ (SAINT+) (Shin et al., 2021) adopted the Transformer
architecture to integrate temporal features, achieving notable improvements in modeling long-

Despite these advances, the sequential DLKT models still primarily rely on sequential ar-
chitectures that emphasize temporal dependencies, and often find it more challenging to explic-
itly model the many-to-many and heterogeneous interactions. Specifically, (i) the relationship
between exercises and KCs is, in many educational domains, a complex many-to-many map-
ping that sequential models (models that process student interactions as ordered temporal se-
quences without explicitly encoding structural relationships among knowledge entities) cannot
adequately represent; (ii) hierarchical dependencies and prerequisite relationships among KCs
are difficult to capture without explicit structural encoding; and (iii) the dynamic evolution of
interactions between students and exercises over time is hard to model within a fixed sequential
framework. These characteristics motivate the need for more expressive modeling approaches
when handling multi-Knowledge-Entity (KE) dependencies, hierarchical Knowledge Structures
(KS), and cross-KC transfer (Yang et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022; Ye and Shan, 2023).
To overcome these limitations, Graph-based Knowledge Tracing (GbKT) was proposed and
has since been actively developed (Nakagawa et al., 2019). GbKT represents exercises, KCs,
and students as nodes in a graph, with edges encoding prerequisite relations, temporal tran-
sitions, and other interactions, and it leverages Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for efficient
information fusion (Abdelrahman and Wang, 2023; Cheng et al., 2024). Graph structures not
only preserve the temporal evolution information of learning behaviors but also flexibly capture
cross-KE and multi-entity dependencies, supporting various educational tasks such as cognitive
path reasoning and personalized diagnosis. Compared to traditional sequential models, it shows
stronger structural modeling capability, interpretability, and scalability (Wang et al., 2023; Gu
et al., 2023).
Although GbKT has seen rapid advancement—driven by a steady flow of new algorithms
proposed by researchers in recent years—most existing reviews still concentrate on the broader
KT landscape; their coverage of GbKT is limited to listing a few representative models without
systematically synthesising the modelling motivations, graph structures, or educational applica-
tion mechanisms involved. Given the expanding body of related work, a comprehensive review
and taxonomy of GbKT has become an urgent need. To address this gap, the present paper
traces the development of GbKT from the viewpoint of graph-structure modelling. We begin
with the modelling motivations, and divide the existing graph structures into two main cate-
gories: knowledge-representation and knowledge-state propagation. On this basis, we further
distil common graph designs and their paradigms, comparing the applicable scenarios and core
mechanisms of different structures. We then analyse how GbKT operates in educational set-
tings and review widely used datasets in terms of model adaptability. Finally, we discuss future
research directions and challenges, and summarise the overall findings.
The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 introduces the research methodology
and literature screening process; Section 3 introduces graph preliminaries and presents the GNN
propagation paradigms under a unified formulation; Section 4 systematically classifies and an-
alyzes graph construction strategies under different modeling motivations; Section 5 elaborates
the application mechanisms and advantages of GbKT in educational tasks; Section 6 reviews
commonly used datasets and analyzes their adaptability; Section 7 discusses future research
directions and summarizes the paper.
The contributions of this paper can be summarized in five key points. First, we present the
first systematic classification of existing GbKT models along two dimensions: modeling moti-
vations and graph structure types. Second, we summarize a unifi

graphs, heterogeneous graphs, hypergraphs, and dynamic graphs, based on the existing GbKT

structure discovery, integration with large models, multimodal graph modeling, and explainabil-
ity for instructional interventions—offering practical guidance for advancing GbKT research.
In this paper, we adopt the following terminology conventions to ensure clarity. A Knowl-
edge Component (KC) refers to the finest-grained unit of knowledge, traditionally called a skill
or concept. A Knowledge Entity (KE) is a general term for any knowledge-bearing node in the
graph; the finest-grained KEs correspond to KCs, but the same schema naturally accommodates
higher-level entities such as domains, subjects, or learning resources. A Knowledge Structure
(KS) refers to the organizational relationships among KEs, such as prerequisite dependencies,
similarity, or hierarchical affiliations, which are typically represented as edges or graph topolo-
gies. For brevity, we refer to a skill/concept simply as aKC, and unify items, questions, or
problems under the single termexercise.
2. SURVEYMETHODS
We followed specific search criteria to select articles relevant to this survey on GbKT, aiming to
comprehensively review recent developments in this field. The criteria were as follows. First,
we conducted literature searches in five major academic databases: ACM Digital Library, IEEE
Xplore, ERIC, Google Scholar, and Web of Science. The search keywords included “knowl-
edge tracing” along with “graph” or “GNN”, and also considered related synonyms such as
“graph-based knowledge tracing.” The time range was limited to 2018–2025 to capture the lat-
est research in recent years. The initial search returned approximately 184 results, which were
reduced to 142 after removing duplicates. Then, by reading titles and abstracts, we excluded
35 articles that were clearly unrelated to the topic or lacked empirical evaluation, leaving 107
for full-text review. Based on further screening, an additional 34 papers were excluded for not
meeting the inclusion criteria, resulting in 73 articles being retained for this survey.
During the screening process, we applied the following main criteria: (1) the model or
method must explicitly adopt a graph-based modeling approach (e.g., using graph neural net-
works, heterogeneous graphs, or knowledge graphs); (2) the article must provide empirical ex-
periments or data analyses, such as evaluating model performance using prediction accuracy,
AUC, or other metrics; (3) the research context must be closely related to student learning or
educational processes. Review papers, studies without accessible full texts, or those that did
not substantially adopt graph-based structures were excluded. By applying these principles, we
aimed to ensure that the selected articles are highly relevant to the theme of GbKT and carry
significant research value.
