A B S T R A C T
Knowledge tracing (KT) is a fundamental task in Intelligent Tutoring Systems, aiming to predict 
learners’ performance on specific questions and trace their evolving knowledge state. With 
the advancement of deep learning in this field, various methods have been applied to model 
the relations between knowledge. However, most existing knowledge tracing methods focus 
on modeling knowledge at a single level, neglecting the inherent hierarchical structure of 
knowledge, which limits their ability to capture complex relations. In this paper, we propose 
a novel hierarchical knowledge tracing model (HKT), which integrates influences of multiple 
knowledge levels to predict learners’ performance. Specifically, we construct different types of 
hierarchical graphs to capture both intra-hierarchy dependencies and cross-hierarchy relations. 
To effectively combine information from multiple levels, we design weight allocation networks 
that dynamically assign weights to different knowledge levels, thereby synthesizing their effects 
for accurate performance prediction. Experimental results demonstrate that HKT outperforms 
baseline methods on multiple benchmark datasets, validating the effectiveness of integrating 
knowledge across all levels compared to single-level models.
1. Introduction
The rapid expansion of online learning platforms has generated an immense volume of learner behavior data, opening up 
new opportunities for the development of Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) (Wang, Lai and Huang, 2024). Effectively managing 
and analyzing this data, as well as accurately assessing a learner’s knowledge state, continues to pose significant challenges for 
ITS. Knowledge Tracing (KT), which draws on educational theory and leverages data-driven methodologies, is designed to model 
cognitive progression and dynamically infer learners’ knowledge states from historical learning data (Abdelrahman, Wang, & Nunes, 
2023; Qian, Zheng, Wang, & Li, 2024). It provides valuable insights and practical tools for making decisions and managing in 
education, serving as a powerful driver for personalized learning (Wang & Sahebi, 2023).
Early KT models based on Hidden Markov Models treat each knowledge concept as an independent variable (Falakmasir, Pardos, 
Gordon, & Brusilovsky, 2013), which can be considered a pointwise model. We refer to it as Pointwise Knowledge Tracing (POI-
KT), as shown in Fig. 1(a). Although the approaches are relatively straightforward to implement, they neglect the interdependencies 
between knowledge concepts, often leading to limited effectiveness in applications (Liu, Hu, Bu, & Yu, 2022). The emergence of deep 
learning technologies motivates researchers to employ more advanced neural network models to enhance the predictive abilities of 
KT (Yang, Yu, Ma, Zhang, & Li, 2022). Whether the backbone network is based on recurrent neural networks (Piech et al., 2015) or

Current research on modeling learners’ knowledge structures has predominantly focused on single-level, planar relations between 
knowledge concepts, without fully considering the complexity of the hierarchical organization and multi-level collaboration of 
knowledge concepts revealed by cognitive architecture theory (Sweller, Van Merrienboer, & Paas, 1998). For example, when a 
learner answers a question related to ‘‘finding a common denominator’’, they not only need to understand this concept but also 
master the higher-level concept of the ‘‘basic properties of fractions’’. Specifically, in order to solve this problem, the learner must 
first understand the ‘‘basic properties of fractions’’ in order to identify that the question is testing ‘‘finding a common denominator’’ 
rather than ‘‘simplifying a fraction’’ and then apply the relevant knowledge to answer correctly. Therefore, multi-level modeling of 
knowledge concepts is crucial for accurately assessing learners’ knowledge structures.
Guided by cognitive architecture theory (Sweller et al., 1998), learners’ cognitive state modeling with the hierarchical structure 
of knowledge concepts involves tackling two key challenges. (1) The difference in the level of abstraction of various knowledge 
concepts makes it a significant challenge to clearly define the hierarchical structure of these concepts within a unified framework. 
(2) Different levels of knowledge have varying impacts on learner responses, making it necessary to assess the influence weight of 
each level. However, existing knowledge tracing (KT) methods typically focus on modeling a single level, neglecting the synergistic 
effects between different levels, which in turn affects the model’s prediction accuracy and generalization ability. Addressing these 
challenges is vital for developing effective models that accurately capture and utilize the hierarchical nature of knowledge concepts.
In this work, we propose the Hierarchical Knowledge Tracing (HKT) model, which explicitly incorporates the hierarchical 
structure of knowledge concepts. It provides a more nuanced understanding of learner performance, distinguishing it from existing 
methods, as depicted in Fig. 1(d). For (1), we introduce a Knowledge Hierarchy Tree (KHT) based on expert prior knowledge, which 
models the vertical hierarchical relationships between knowledge concepts and establishes a top-down knowledge organization 
framework. Meanwhile, we construct a Knowledge Association Graph (KAG) using learning sequence information to represent 
interrelations between concepts at the same hierarchical level. Subsequently, we construct hierarchical embedding representations 
that not only indicate the level of each knowledge concept but also capture the distances between levels. These embeddings are 
further refined through the application of a cross-graph, hierarchical contrastive learning strategy. For (2), we propose weight 
allocation networks that can dynamically assess the impact of different levels of knowledge concepts on prediction results. By 
adaptively assigning different levels of weights based on the relevance of the prediction task, the model can provide a comprehensive 
evaluation of the learner’s responses. Experimental results based on real-world datasets show a significant improvement in predictive 
performance. Furthermore, our approach exhibits strong interpretability. The explicit modeling of knowledge concepts using KHT
