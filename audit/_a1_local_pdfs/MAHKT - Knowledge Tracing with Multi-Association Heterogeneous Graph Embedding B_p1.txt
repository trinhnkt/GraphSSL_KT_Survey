A B S T R A C T
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is crucial in online education for tracing learners knowledge states and improving
learning outcomes. However, existing graph-based KT models often focus solely on prerequisite associations
among concepts or correlations between concepts and questions, neglecting the complex multi-association
relationships among entities. This oversight causes biased question representations, which in turn affects the
knowledge transfer propagation process, ultimately leading to inconsistencies between the represented and
actual knowledge states. To address this, we propose multi-association heterogeneous knowledge transfer
(MAHKT), a novel KT model that leverages multi-association heterogeneous graph embeddings based on
knowledge-transfer principles, to better capture the evolution of knowledge states. Five types of associations
were defined between questions and concepts to construct a multi-association heterogeneous graph, thereby
proposing a knowledge triplet embedding method to extract the potential value of these associations.
Grounded in near- and far-transfer theories, MAHKT employs a perception-attention mechanism to calculate
similarity weights, propagates embeddings from first-order neighbors to capture near-transfer effects, and stacks
propagation layers to aggregate far-transfer information, thereby comprehensively integrating richer knowledge
from similar knowledge transfers across various association contexts. Finally, two LSTM modules were utilized
to track the evolution of learner’s knowledge states across questions and concepts, to predict their future
performance. The experimental results show that MAHKT significantly outperformed existing KT methods,
enhancing prediction accuracy and improving model interpretability in the knowledge transfer process.
1. Introduction
In recent years, the rapid growth and widespread adoption of online
education platforms have transformed learning, offering significant
convenience to teachers and learners [1–3]. With the rise of artificial
intelligence, these platforms now enable intelligent resource recom-
mendations [4] and personalized teaching interventions [5], such as
intelligent tutoring systems and massive open online courses, making
them a crucial part of modern education [6].
Knowledge tracing (KT) is a crucial technology that has been widely
integrated into online education platforms in data-driven intelligent
education. The primary aim of KT is to effectively monitor learners’
knowledge states by analyzing the temporal response data from their
learning processes, enabling the prediction of their future performance,

transfer further negatively impacts the model’s predictive performance.
While addressing the inconsistency in knowledge state representa-
tions is crucial for the KT task, two major challenges must be overcome
to achieve this goal. On the one hand, the representation of questions
depends on complex associations with concepts. In fact, as depicted in
Fig. 1(b), the knowledge structure can be modeled as a heterogeneous
graph with five types of associations: prerequisite and similarity asso-
ciations among questions and among concepts, as well as correlation
associations between questions and concepts. As shown in Fig. 1(b),
the question 𝑞1(3 + 4 =?) serves as a prerequisite for question 𝑞2((3 +

𝑐2(𝑀 𝑢𝑙 𝑡𝑖𝑝𝑙 𝑖𝑐 𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛); 𝑐1 shows case similarity to concept 𝑐3(𝑆 𝑢𝑏𝑡𝑟𝑎𝑐 𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛).
Moreover, a correlation exists between concept 𝑐1 and question 𝑞1.
Mining these interrelated data can assist with the comprehension of
association between questions and concepts, improving the perfor-
mance of the KT model. However, inadequate association capture or
integration can lead to deviations in the overall semantic features of
the question representation. For instance, there is the similarity asso-
ciation between questions 𝑞1 and 𝑞3. When this similarity association
is ignored, questions 𝑞1 and 𝑞3 may be represented as independent
representation vectors, failing to reflect their inherent semantic connec-
tion. Such biased question representations hinder the model’s ability to
accurately trace knowledge states, particularly when representing sim-
ilar questions, thereby reducing predictive accuracy. Therefore, mining
and integrating these complex associations is crucial to improving the
performance of KT methods.
On the other hand, knowledge propagation follows near- and far-
transfer theories [14,15], which depends on the similarity and strength
of associations between entities. According to the common elements
theory [16], knowledge transfer is more likely when two entities share
common features. The more the similarities, the higher is the like-
lihood of near transfer; when similarities are fewer, far transfer oc-
curs [17]. Near transfer involves sharing information across similar
domains through direct associations, such as correlation, similarity, or
prerequisites. As shown in Fig. 1(c) (left part), concept 𝑐1 is directly
linked to concepts (𝑐2, 𝑐3, 𝑐4) and questions (𝑞1, 𝑞2), necessitating the
aggregation of these features. Near transfer is generally more com-
mon [18] than far transfer although the latter has been validated by
many scholars [15,19,20]. Far transfer occurs across less related do-
mains, where indirect associations exert a weaker influence. In Fig. 1(c)
(right part), concept 𝑐1 is indirectly linked to questions 𝑞3 and 𝑞4,
indirectly influencing the current concept, which is the example of the
far transfer. A comprehensive question representation must integrate
these varied associations; however, developing effective methods for
this purpose remains a challenge. When complex associations, such as
prerequisite and similarity associations, are overlooked, the knowledge
information of related nodes cannot be appropriately updated and
aggregated during knowledge propagation. For instance, if the prereq-
uisite association between concepts 𝑐1 and 𝑐4 is ignored, not only the
direct adjacent prerequisite features cannot be aggregated effectively,
but also the indirect association between 𝑐1 and question 𝑞4 cannot be
captured. This will lead to inappropriate updating and aggregation of 𝑐1
during both the near and far transfer processes, resulting in incomplete
question representation. As a result, this exacerbates the inconsistencies
in knowledge state updates and further degrades model performance.

heterogeneous question graph through near and far transfers, overcom-
ing the limitations of current models. We categorized these associations
into five types: prerequisites and similarities among questions, prereq-
uisites and similarities among concepts, and correlations between ques-
