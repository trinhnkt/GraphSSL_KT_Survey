Abstract
Intelligent technology plays a pivotal role in revolutionizing learning assess-
ments, overcoming the constraints of traditional assessment methods and driving 
educational innovation. Knowledge tracing (KT) emerges as a critical component 
for assessing students’ learning states and forecasting their future performance. 
However, existing graph-based KT models often ignore certain real-world factors 
(e.g., sequence dependencies, question information, and hierarchical relationships 
between knowledge concepts). These factors hinder the model’s overall performance 
in predicting students’ learning progress and identifying their knowledge deficien-
cies, thus limiting the model’s applicability in personalized learning. To address 
these challenges, we propose a novel graph-based effective knowledge tracing model 
via subject knowledge mapping (SGKT). We begin by extracting essential features 
from historical learning interactions, which include the relationships between ques-
tions, the difficulty of questions, and the relationships between knowledge concepts. 
These knowledge concepts are seamlessly integrated with a purpose-designed sub-
ject knowledge mapping, establishing intricate hierarchical relations. Subsequently, 
we employ graph convolutional networks to aggregate the embeddings of questions 
and knowledge concepts, thereby optimizing the update process for predicting stu-
dents’ knowledge states. Experimental validation on the dataset demonstrates the 
superior performance of our model, surpassing the original KT model and other rel-
evant approaches by up to 5% in terms of the area under the curve and accuracy. 
These results not only demonstrate the efficacy of our proposed model but also pro-
vide valuable insights for advancing learning assessments.

1  Introduction
UNESCO, in Education 2030 Framework for Action, underscores education’s 
commitment to individuals’ holistic development (Mundial, 2016). Personal-
ized education hinges on students understanding their knowledge mastery levels 
(Williams & Zipser, 1989; Hooshyar, 2024). As Ausubel et al. (1978) noted, "the 
single most important factor shaping the learning process is what the student is 
already aware of". The ascent of personalized learning is propelled by techno-
logical advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI), big data, and the Internet of 
Things (IoT). In this technological environment, Knowledge tracing (KT) tech-
niques have emerged in the realm of human–computer interaction, designed to 
simulate the dynamic interactions between students and the exercise content they 
engage with. This simulation enables accurate, real-time assessment and predic-
tion of students’ changing knowledge mastery levels (Abdelrahman et al., 2023). 
Consequently, students can address weaknesses, identify areas for improvement, 
and choose suitable methods and resources for learning. Simultaneously, teachers 
can dynamically tailor teaching activities based on the insights provided by KT, 
ensuring that classroom designs are in harmony with the actual learning condi-
tions of students, thereby fulfilling the goals of teaching support.
The growth of personalized learning underscores the imperative to identify and 
dynamically assess students’ knowledge states (Murtaza et al., 2022). Given that 
students’ knowledge states are often implicit, direct assessment poses challenges 
(Ma et  al., 2023). Figure  1 depicts the shift from "one-dimensional static out-
come-based diagnosis" to "multi-dimensional dynamic process-based Tracing". 
Initially, Classical Test Theory (CTT) assessed students’ ability ratings directly 
through fixed exams and their corresponding scores (Gulliksen, 2013). How-
ever, the approach of directly linking constant difficulty levels of each exam with 
ability scores lacks scientific rigor. To address this issue, Item Response Theory 
(IRT) emerged, facilitating the estimation of mastery stages of latent-knowledge 
(Maghsudi et al., 2021; Traub, 1981). IRT considers students’ abilities and test 
paper conditions, creating a functional relation. However, relying solely on a 
standard total score still fails to characterize students’ mastery of various knowl-
edge concepts due to individual cognitive variances. This implies that even if the 
total scores are the same, there can be significant differences in students’ under-
standing of specific knowledge concepts.
In the realm of assessment theory, a new paradigm emerged with the creation 
of cognitive diagnostic theory, integrating psychological assessment and cogni-
tive science (Ackerman, 2014; Lubis & Yus, 2024). Gierl et al. (2008) utilized 
cognitive attribute diagnostics to uncover variations in students’ arithmetic 
scores, offering comprehensive assessments of algebraic understanding. Static 
cognitive diagnosis suits situations like exams, providing in-depth assessments 
of learning data within a specific time-frame, indicating the current states of 
knowledge mastery. However, real learning unfolds gradually over an extended
