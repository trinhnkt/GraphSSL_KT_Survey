A B S T R A C T   
Knowledge tracing (KT), in which the future performance of students is estimated by tracing their knowledge 
states based on their responses to exercises, is widely applied in the field of intelligent education. However, 
existing mainstream KT models explore the importance of knowledge relations but ignore the key role of 
cognitive factors. According to the knowledge construction theory, the human cognitive system performs both 
spatial accommodation and temporal assimilation to internalize knowledge. In this paper, we propose an 
innovative heterogeneous graph-based Knowledge tracing method with spatiotemporal evolution (TSKT), in 
which knowledge state evolution is traced along both temporal and spatial dimensions. We construct a het­
erogeneous graph with multiple exercise attributes, including content, concepts, and difficulty, to obtain a 
knowledge space with richer exercise representations through hierarchical aggregation. We design a spatial 
updating module in which each interaction updates the current node’s state of the knowledge space and transfers 
its influence to its neighbors. We also design a temporal updating module to further update the knowledge state 
through short-term memory enhancing and long-term memory forgetting. Finally, we stack these modules to 
obtain deeper features by using alternate spatiotemporal updating. Extensive experiments on three datasets 
reveal the superiority of the proposed method and its variants in terms of future performance prediction.   
1. Introduction 
With the current continuous development in information and 
Internet technology, online education providers such as Khan Academy, 
Assistment, and Coursera have developed a large body of teaching re­
sources that can overcome the time and space limitations faced by 
several students (Zhuang et al., 2022). Therefore, establishing a scien­
tific measurement method to trace the knowledge status of students is a 
key issue for appropriate learning material suggestion and personalized 
learning path recommendation (Hooshyar et al., 2022) in intelligent 
tutoring systems, and this aspect has been attracting growing attention 
from the public (Gao et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2019). 
Knowledge tracing (KT) (Liu et al., 2021) aims to use time-series data 
obtained from student answers to exercises in the learning process to 
model knowledge mastery and predict future performance, as illustrated 
in Fig. 1(a). For example, deep knowledge tracing (DKT) (Piech et al., 
2015) and dynamic key-value memory networks (DKVMN) (Zhang et al., 
2017) use the hidden vectors of a recurrent neural network (RNN) and 
memory matrix, respectively, to trace the evolution of knowledge in the 
temporal dimension. Other studies have focused on the impact of 
forgetting factors on the knowledge state (Ghosh et al., 2020; Nagatani 
et al., 2019). In addition to the temporal dimension, an increasing 
number of KT methods (Nakagawa et al., 2019; Tong et al., 2020a, 
2020b) have emphasized the importance of spatial dimension relation­
ships between exercises and knowledge concepts. SKT (Tong et al., 
2020a, 2020b) involved the use of an exercise-knowledge hierarchical 
graph neural network to describe these relationships. GKT (Nakagawa 
et al., 2019) required the construction of a homogeneous graph using 
prerequisite relations to represent the relation between knowledge 
concepts. JKT (Song et al., 2021) employed a joint graph convolutional

ignored the spatiotemporal evolution of the knowledge states of

According to the knowledge construction theory adopted in the field 
of education (Kimmerle et al., 2010, 2015; Piaget, 1970, 1977), when 
students arrive at a new exercise, their cognitive system does not 
internalize knowledge by simply transferring and integrating the infor­
mation (Piaget, 1970). Here, knowledge internalization is an active 
process of adding new content (assimilation) and changing the structure 
of the knowledge (accommodation) (Kimmerle et al., 2010). Assimila­
tion and accommodation are natural cognitive laws that cause a stu­
dent’s knowledge state to change over time and space. Assimilation 
occurs in the temporal dimension when students arrive at new exercises, 
ultimately adding new pieces of information to their prior knowledge 
(Kimmerle et al., 2010). For example, students answering a new exer­
cise, e2, at time t2 will experience decays in their knowledge state 
depending on the time interval, the degree of assimilation of their old 
knowledge (Piaget, 1977), and updating of their current knowledge 
state in the temporal dimension. Meanwhile, accommodation occurs in 
the spatial dimension when a student acquires knowledge and transfers 
it to other associated knowledge. For example, as shown in Fig. 1(c), 
when a student interacts with exercise e1, the knowledge states of 
related concepts k2 and k3 are updated, and the effect is transferred to 
similar knowledge via accommodation (Piaget, 1970). Consequently, 
according to the knowledge construction process that occurs with ex­
ercise answering, the modeling of KT must consider accommodation 
with transfer and assimilation with the reception of knowledge in both 
the temporal and spatial dimensions. 
However, two main challenges are associated with this approach. 
First, the evolution of a student’s knowledge state follows the mecha­
nism of assimilation and accommodation based on the knowledge con­
struction theory. As depicted in Fig. 1(c), a student’s knowledge 
undergoes assimilation over time on the temporal axis, while on the 
spatial axis the knowledge status is accommodated by neighboring 
concepts within the knowledge structure. Specifically, for the spatial 
evolution, the state changes of each knowledge concept within the 
knowledge space are transferred to adjacent concepts. For instance, 
when a particular exercise e1 is answered correctly (or incorrectly), the 
student’s proficiency of k1 increases (or decreases). This effect will be 
transferred to its neighbors, that is, k2 and k3, as well as to higher-level 
associated knowledge concepts. Evidently, at time t1, the student’s 
mastery of knowledge concepts k2 and k3 have both diminished from 
their initial default value of 1. Moreover, for the temporal evolution, 
knowledge can only be assimilated and received if it is not forgotten over 
time. Considerably, at time t2, as time advances, the student’s profi­
ciency in a specific knowledge concept (i.e., k3 or k4) has decreased, 
indicating forgetting without being assimilated or absorbed. However, 
forgetting is influenced by a variety of factors including the frequency of 
applying the same knowledge, and it is often difficult for a general RNN 
(Piech et al., 2015) to capture the influence of different time lengths on 
forgetting. Therefore, it is necessary to portray the assimilation and 
accommodation processes of knowledge in both the temporal and spatial 
dimensions. 
Second, knowledge construction in temporal and spatial evolution is 
dependent on the relationship between exercises and knowledge con­
cepts. The knowledge space theory (Doignon & Falmagne, 1985) asserts 
that an exercise is not isolated; instead, there exist relations between 
exercises resulting from prerequisite relations between knowledge 
(Chen et al., 2018). In fact, each exercise contains a variety of hetero­
geneous characteristics such as content q, knowledge concept c, and 
difficulty d. The exercise heterogeneous graph network comprises mul­
tiple heterogeneous nodes, as shown in Fig. 1(b). Consequently, a 
comprehensive exercise representation necessitates the fusion of diverse 
exercises and their heterogeneous attributes. Nonetheless, developing 
methodologies that effectively harness these heterogeneous attributes to 
create accurate exercise representations and capture their implicit 
knowledge relationships presents a significant challenge. 
The primary aim of our research is to calculate students’ knowledge 
states by tracing the processes of assimilation and accommodation to 
address the inadequate knowledge construction in traditional models. 
Consequently, we divide the knowledge modeling process into two di­
mensions: temporal and spatial. Existing KT methods mainly focus on 
updating knowledge in the temporal dimension, lacking consideration 
of spatial influences that result in limited knowledge propagation. To 
address this challenge, we propose a novel heterogeneous graph-based 
KT method with temporal–spatial evolution (TSKT) to update and 
trace students’ knowledge states over time and space. As for the spatial 
dimension’s accommodation process, we extend the traditional homo­
geneous graph-based knowledge propagation approach (Tong et al., 
2020a, 2020b) by employing graph neural networks to aggregate multi- 
dimensional heterogeneous information from exercises, thereby 
enriching the knowledge representation of exercises. Additionally,
