A B S T R A C T
Recently the large-scale influence of Covid-19 promoted the fast development of intelligent tutoring systems
(ITS). As a major task of ITS, Knowledge Tracing (KT) aims to capture a student’s dynamic knowledge state
based on his historical response sequences and provide personalized learning assistance to him. However,
most existing KT methods have encountered the data sparsity problem. In real scenarios, an online tutoring
system usually has an extensive collection of questions while each student can only interact with a limited
number of questions. As a result, the records of some questions could be extremely sparse, which degrades the
performance of traditional KT models. To resolve this issue, we propose a Dual-channel Heterogeneous Graph
Network (DHGN) to learn informative representations of questions from students’ records by capturing both the
high-order heterogeneous and local relations. As the supervised learning manner applied in previous methods
is incapable of exploiting unobserved relations between questions, we innovatively integrate a self-supervised
framework into the KT task and employ contrastive learning via the two channels of DHGN, supplementing
as an auxiliary task to improve the KT performance. Moreover, we adopt the attention mechanism, which
has achieved impressive performance in natural language processing tasks, to effectively capture students’
knowledge state. But the standard attention network is inapplicable to the KT task because the current
knowledge state of a student usually shows strong dependency on his recently interactive questions, unlike
the situation of language processing tasks, which focus more on the long-term dependency. To avoid the
inefficiency of standard attention networks in the KT task, we further devise a novel Hybrid Attentive Network
(HAN), which produces both the global attention and the hierarchical local attention to model the long-
term and short-term intents, respectively. Then, by the gating network, a student’s long-term and short-term
intents are combined for efficient prediction. We conduct extensive experiments on several real-world datasets.
Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed methods achieve significant performance improvement
compared to existing state-of-the-art baselines, which validates the effectiveness of the proposed dual-channel
heterogeneous graph framework and hybrid attentive network.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of adaptive learning, intelligent tutor-
ing systems (ITS) have found more and more applications. ITS may
not require the knowledge and experience of education experts as
before. By analyzing a student’s historical records, ITS can effectively
track the dynamic change of his knowledge state. Nowadays knowledge
tracing (KT) (Corbett & Anderson, 1994) has become a hot research
topic of ITS, which aims to analyze a student’s knowledge state and
predict his response to a new question. According to the prediction
results, teachers can adjust the teaching plans and develop personalized
strategies (Adesope et al., 2017) for each student in online education
systems.

KT research can be dated back to the late 1960s. At the early stage,
some novel Bayesian modeling methods (Pozna et al., 2010; Yudelson
et al., 2013) were developed to make great progress in the heuristic
modeling. Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) (Kasurinen & Nikula,
2009) is a typical class of methods. BKT represents a student’s knowl-
edge acquisition level with a binary number (1 for mastery and 0 for
not mastery). Later, multiple factors are taken to construct a student’s
knowledge state. The Difficulty, Ability, and Student History (DASH)
model (Lindsey et al., 2014) considers the impact of additional factors,
such as question difficulty, error rate, response time, on students’ final
response. However, the above models strongly depend on the statistical
estimation of parameters and are not robust enough.

Over the past decade, there has emerged some attempts to construct
nonlinear modeling approaches (Fersini et al., 2014; Hedrea & Petriu,
2021), which achieved superior performance in diverse fields. Along
this trend, neural network-based approaches were applied to knowl-
edge tracing. Due to their strong robustness and portability, neural
network-based methods generally perform better than traditional ones.
For example, Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) (Piech et al., 2015) first
utilizes the long short term memory (LSTM) network (Hochreiter &
Schmidhuber, 1997) to track a student’s state evolution process. Ensem-
ble Knowledge Tracing (EKT) (Sun et al., 2022) introduces an ensemble
learning into KT, which fuses BKT and DKT, and extends AdaBoost to
deal with KT sequential data. But this recurrent network architecture is
insufficient to capture long-term dependencies and is easy to lose early
semantic details. Then some memory-based methods (Abdelrahman
& Wang, 2019; Liu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2017), like Dynamic
Key-Value Memory Networks for Knowledge Tracing (DKVMN) (Zhang
et al., 2017), were proposed to resolve the above issues by introducing
an external memory matrix.
Later, attention-based methods (Vaswani et al., 2017) have made
great advances in handling sequential data, which can automatically
study different weights for each interaction to capture the related
information in the historical sequences. The Self-Attentive Knowledge
Tracing (SAKT) (Pandey & Karypis, 2019) first utilizes the attention
mechanism to focus on the historical interactions that are highly rel-
evant to the target question. However, due to the long recording
cycle of KT, the traditional attention mechanism does not perform
well. Context-Aware Attentive Knowledge Tracing (AKT) (Ghosh et al.,
2020) introduces a monotonic attention mechanism that integrates the
exponential decay and characterizes the importance of memory decay
in KT.
Recently, graph neural network (GNN) (Dong et al., 2023, 2022;
Kipf & Welling, 2016; Schlichtkrull et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2019)
shows superior performance on the relation network and graph-
structured data, and can encode rich context information by aggre-
gating graph attributive and structural features. Some GNN-based
methods (Ni et al., 2023; Song et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022; Wu
et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2020) have been leveraged in the KT field, like
A Graph-based Interaction Model for Knowledge Tracing (GIKT) (Yang
et al., 2020), which combines the latent relations between questions
and skills by utilizing the graph convolutional network (GCN) (Kipf
& Welling, 2016). A joint graph convolutional network based Deep
Knowledge Tracing (JKT) (Song et al., 2021) models the multi-
dimensional relationships of ‘‘exercise-to-exercise’’ and ‘‘concept-to-
concept’’ via two graph networks.
Although existing methods have achieved significant improvement
in knowledge tracing, they still suffer from the following limitations.
1. The performance of those methods mainly relies on the scale of
historical interactions. However, due to the sparsity of records
in KT, the visiting frequency of some questions is extremely
low. To avoid over-fitting and over-parameterization, previous
methods (Ghosh et al., 2020; Piech et al., 2015; Zhang et al.,
2017) usually select the skill records, instead of the question
records (the number of questions is generally much larger than
that of skills), to model a student’s knowledge state. But this
usually leads to the loss of semantic details being contained in
historical interactions .
2. Some graph-based methods (Song et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2020;
Yang et al., 2020) do improve the representations of questions by
fusing the graph-structured information, but may mostly over-
look the heterogeneity of nodes. In KT, these recorded questions
usually attach diverse attributes, like question type, difficulty
coefficient, involved skill, etc. Obviously, simple homogeneous
graphs (question-skill pair) are incapable of characterizing het-
erogeneous relations and building expressive representations for
questions.
3. Traditional attention-based methods often yield poor perfor-
mance in long sequence prediction (Pandey & Karypis, 2019). In
fact, memory decays over time, i.e., the knowledge, which was
well mastered before, may be forgotten now (Carpenter et al.,
2008; Nagatani et al., 2019). It is non-trivial to balance the
effects of short-term and long-term memory in student modeling.
To address the above limitations, we propose a novel knowledge
tracing model, a Hybrid Attentive Network fused with Dual-channel
Heterogeneous Graph Network (HAN-DHGN). First, we introduce a
dual-channel heterogeneous graph network (DHGN) and then learn
question embeddings via the network schema and meta-path chan-
nels of DHGN, which can effectively exploit the local and high-order
relations between questions. As most KT methods are based on a
supervised learning paradigm, which is limited by the data sparsity
in real scenarios, making it incapable to learn quality representa-
tions, we introduce self-supervised learning into DHGN, aiming to
exploit unobserved relations and reinforce the question representations.
Specifically, we utilize the question representations learned by the two
channels of DHGN to conduct contrastive learning, which maximizes
the similarity between positive pairs while minimizing the similarity
between negative-sampled counterparts. To the best of our knowledge,
the potential of contrastive learning for heterogeneous graph repre-
sentation learning and KT has not yet been explored. Therefore, our
work aims to fill this research gap and investigate the effectiveness of
contrastive learning in the KT field.
Moreover, to improve the efficiency of traditional attention-based
models and perceive the dynamics of memory, we propose a novel
hybrid attentive network (HAN), including both global attention and
hierarchical local attention, to track a student’s intents in the long
and short terms, respectively. In HAN, the global attention deals with
the whole interactive sequences to capture the long-term intent. To
characterize the memory decay in the long sequence, we add an ex-
ponential decay to the standard attention network. The local attention
focuses on the recent interactive details by a cluster of hierarchical
attention windows. As the window size increases with the number
of layers, our model can perceive the relevance in longer scope and
extract multi-scale semantics features. The hierarchical construction
greatly optimizes the short-term intent modeling. Then the two intents
are adaptively integrated via a gating network for the prediction task.
Finally, we unify the prediction task and the self-supervised task, and
leverage the multi-task training strategy to jointly optimize the two
tasks. Our contributions can be summarized as follows.
∙We propose a novel dual-channel heterogeneous graph network
(DHGN) for representation learning. The dual-channel framework can
effectively exploit the local and high-order relations between questions,
helping the model build expressive question representations for the
downstream prediction task. Although the existing GNN-based meth-
ods demonstrate their superiority in the KT field, most of them are
built upon a simple homogeneous graph, which neglects the complex
heterogeneity between questions and may consequently fail to learn
expressive representations for questions.
∙We devise a novel hybrid attentive network (HAN) to capture a
student’s long-term and short-term intents simultaneously. The conven-
tional attention mechanism, which focuses on long-term dependencies,
is incapable of modeling memory decay in real scenarios and often
yields poor performance. Compared with the conventional attention
mechanism, our HAN fuses global attention and hierarchical local
attention, aiming to model a student’s state in a more effective manner,
and achieves decent gains.
