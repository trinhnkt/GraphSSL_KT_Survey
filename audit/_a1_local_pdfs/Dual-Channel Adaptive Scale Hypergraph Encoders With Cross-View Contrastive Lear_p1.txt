Dual-Channel Adaptive Scale Hypergraph Encoders
With Cross-View Contrastive Learning
for Knowledge Tracing
Jiawei Li , Yuanfei Deng , Yixiu Qin , Shun Mao , and Yuncheng Jiang , Member, IEEE
Abstract— Knowledge tracing (KT) refers to predicting learn-
ers’ performance in the future according to their historical
responses, which has become an essential task in intelligent
tutoring systems. Most deep learning-based methods usually
model the learners’ knowledge states via recurrent neural
networks (RNNs) or attention mechanisms. Recently emerging
graph neural networks (GNNs) assist the KT model to capture
the relationships such as question–skill and question–learner.
However, non-pairwise and complex higher-order information
among responses is ignored. In addition, a single-channel encoded
hidden vector struggles to represent multigranularity knowledge
states. To tackle the above problems, we propose a novel KT
model named dual-channel adaptive scale hypergraph encoders
with cross-view contrastive learning (HyperKT). Specifically,
we design an adaptive scale hyperedge distillation component
for generating knowledge-aware hyperedges and pattern-aware
hyperedges that reflect non-pairwise higher-order features among
responses. Then, we propose dual-channel hypergraph encoders
to capture multigranularity knowledge states from global and
local state hypergraphs. The encoders consist of a simplified
hypergraph convolution network and a collaborative hypergraph
convolution network. To enhance the supervisory signal in the
state hypergraphs, we introduce the cross-view contrastive learn-
ing mechanism, which performs among state hypergraph views
and their transformed line graph views. Extensive experiments on
three real-world datasets demonstrate the superior performance
of our HyperKT over the state-of-the-art (SOTA).
Index Terms— Contrastive learning, hyperedge distillation,
hypergraph learning, knowledge tracing (KT).

I. INTRODUCTION
W
ITH the rapid expansion of online intelligent tutoring
systems, there has been an exponential increase in the
abundance of question resources and learner behavior data [1].
Assessing these data proves instrumental in enabling learners
to accurately understand their strengths and weaknesses, con-
sequently facilitating the development of personalized learning
paths [2]. As an effective technology for educational data
mining, knowledge tracing (KT) lays the foundation for
human cognitive states tracing, adaptive learning systems, and
exercise recommendations [3], [4]. The KT model aims to
accurately predict learners’ future performance by tracing the
knowledge mastery states [5], [6]. Specifically, the KT model
predicts the probability of learners correctly answering the
next question based on their historical response records.
The existing KT models based on Bayesian [7] and factor
analysis [8] represent typical methods that have consistently
demonstrated strong performance. Leveraging the powerful
representation capabilities of deep learning, there is a dis-
cernible trend toward KT research based on deep learning
methods [9], [10]. To acquire the hidden knowledge state, early

deep learning-based KT approaches encode learners’ response
sequences via recurrent neural networks (RNNs) [11], memory
networks [12], and attention mechanism [9]. Recent stud-
ies have focused on modeling diverse learning behaviors
from response sequences, which are beneficial to improving
the performance of the KT model, such as the forgetting
mechanism [13], [14], learner individualization [15], temporal
cross-effects [16], and learning gains [14].
While optimizing the modeling of the learning process and
learning behavior can enhance prediction performance, a sig-
nificant challenge arises from the sparse interaction between
learners and a large number of questions [17]. To address the
data sparsity issue, some approaches aim to incorporate textual
information of questions into the KT model [6]. However, the
textual content of questions is often challenging to obtain [10].
Recently, graph neural networks (GNNs) have demonstrated
notable success in modeling graph structural data [18].
Graph-based KT models have exhibited remarkable perfor-
mance, utilizing message-passing on question–skill graphs to
refine question embeddings [19], [20]. Other graph-based KT
methods focus on modeling latent knowledge states from
learner–question interaction graphs [21].
However, a significant limitation of existing KT models is
that they only utilize pairwise relationships to model knowl-
edge states, overlooking higher-order relationship patterns
prevalent among responses [22]. For instance, the non-pairwise
high-order relationship among responses of interacting sub-
sequence reflects the learner’s knowledge state within that
time span. Unlike graph-based models that emphasize pairwise
relationships, hypergraph generalizes edges to connect more
than two nodes [23], [24], offering a natural manner to
capture complex higher-order relationships among responses.
While the incorporation of hypergraphs to model higher-order
relationships among responses is advantageous for inferring
learners’ knowledge states, generating effective hyperedges
poses a challenge [25]. In addition, existing KT methods
typically encode the knowledge state in a single channel [26],
resulting in the inability to model the multigranular knowledge
states during the learning process. In fact, response sequences
under different views represent diverse knowledge states of
learners.
To
address
the
above
issues,
this
article
proposes
dual-channel
adaptive
scale
hypergraph
encoders
with
cross-view contrastive learning for KT (HyperKT), which
consists of three key learning components. First, to cap-
ture the non-pairwise and complex high-order relationships
among responses, we propose a hyperedge distillation com-
ponent for constructing state hypergraphs. Leveraging prior
knowledge of problem skills and learning patterns during
the learning process, hyperedge distillation adaptively extracts
knowledge-aware hyperedges and pattern-aware hyperedges
from response sequences. A distillation matrix is obtained
through hyperedge distillation, facilitating the capture of
essential information transmission between nodes and hyper-
edges in the hypergraph. Then, we construct global and
local state hypergraphs, which are encoded via introduced
dual-channel hypergraph encoders to capture multigranu-
larity knowledge states. One channel of the hypergraph
encoder employs a simplified hypergraph convolution net-
work, efficiently modeling learners’ global knowledge states.
In particular, for the local state hypergraph encoder channel,
we propose a collaborative hypergraph convolution network to
model fine-grained local knowledge states, with collaborative
information comprising hyperedge distillation matrices and
attention weight matrices. Finally, to address the issue of
insufficient supervision signals in the state hypergraph caused
by the sparsity of response sequences, we design a cross-view
hypergraph contrastive learning mechanism, performing local
and global contrastive learning between two hypergraph views
and their transformed line graph views. The cross-view con-
trastive learning on the state hypergraphs maximizes the
mutual information between the learned local knowledge state
and the global knowledge state.
The main contributions of the proposed HyperKT are sum-
marized as follows.
1) To capture non-pairwise high-order relationships among
responses, we propose a hyperedge distillation compo-
nent to guide the generation of knowledge-aware and
pattern-aware hyperedges from response sequences.
2) We design dual-channel hypergraph encoders to model
multigranular knowledge states from global and local
state hypergraphs, including simplified and collaborative
hypergraph convolution channels.
3) To enhance self-supervised signals in hypergraphs,
we introduce a cross-view contrastive learning mech-
anism, maximizing the mutual information of the global
and local knowledge states learned from multiviews.
4) Extensive experiments on three public datasets demon-
strate that the proposed HyperKT outperforms various
state-of-the-art (SOTA) models.
The remainder of this article is organized as follows.
Section II reviews the relevant works of this article. Sec-
tions III and IV describe the problem definition and the
details of the proposed HyperKT. The experimental results and
analyses are presented in Section V. Section VI concludes this
article.
II. RELATED WORKS
A. Knowledge Tracing
The goal of KT is to trace learners’ changing knowledge
states during exercise and correctly predict their performance
on the subsequent questions. Existing KT methods can be
categorized into traditional machine learning-based methods
and deep learning-based methods.
