6. Conclusion 
In this paper, we explore predicting future performance by tracing 
knowledge state evolution, guided by knowledge construction theory. 
Existing KT methods often overlook the influence of accommodation 
and assimilation on student responses, resulting in inadequate modeling 
of knowledge construction. To overcome this, we propose TSKT, a novel 
KT method that improves interpretability and performance. TSKT 
simultaneously traces knowledge state evolution in students’ learning 
process along both temporal and spatial dimensions. It employs an ex­
ercise heterogeneous graph to enhance exercise representations, 
creating a new knowledge space with diverse exercise information and 
relationships. We further propose a spatial updating module that utilizes 
a graph neural network to update node state by current interactions and 
then transfers each node’s influence to neighboring nodes, capturing the 
accommodation influence within the knowledge space. Finally, we 
construct a temporal updating module for updating the knowledge state 
through short-term memory updating and long-term memory forgetting 
to capture the assimilation influence of the knowledge mastery level. 
The spatiotemporal updating mechanism can effectively simulate the 
process of student knowledge state accommodation and assimilation 
process based on knowledge constructive theory. Extensive experiments 
on real-world datasets demonstrate that TSKT outperforms existing KT 
methods in prediction accuracy. 
Although our TSKT model obtains good performance, its practical 
application still suffers from some limitations. The TSKT model can trace 
the evolution of students’ knowledge status in the learning process along 
the temporal and spatial dimensions. However, the construction of the 
heterogeneous graph of exercises depends on the multi-dimensional 
exercise features, and the spatiotemporal updating module also needs 
spatiotemporal response information. Considering that we cannot cap­
ture all these features in every scenario, this leads to the limitations in 
the generality and applicability of the TSKT model in various scenarios 
or datasets. Therefore, we will further improve the general applicability 
and practical application of our model in future work, improving the 
application prospects of our TSKT model. On the one hand, we intend to 
leverage common features to reduce the demand for special features, 
thereby expanding the model’s suitability across various datasets. This 
endeavor will facilitate the widespread adoption of our model, extend­
ing its application within a broader educational landscape. On the other 
hand, we will employ advanced analytical technologies, such as causal 
analysis (Yang et al., 2022), to identify pivotal features in the learning 
process while eliminating redundant or trivial attributes. Moreover, 
with the widespread application of smart education systems and 
development of artificial intelligence technology, the accompanying 
collection in learning process will be gradually realized (Liu et al., 
2022). This development will not only promote the enrichment of 
collected features but also provide more possibilities for our future 
model research and verification. This trend contributes to the augmen­
tation of our feature collection and offers expanded perspectives for our 
forthcoming model research. 
Of course, there are some important directions worth exploring in 
the future. First, we recognize the existence of diverse relationships and 
structure among exercises, knowledge concepts, and students, including 
predecessor and sibling relations of concept-concept (Song et al., 2021), 
exercise-concept associations (Song et al., 2022), and multi-level re­
lationships of student-exercise-concept (Sun et al., 2022). Further 
consideration of these entity relationships can enhance knowledge 
representation and render the spatiotemporal update module more 
reasonable and explicable. Second, we aim to explore the incorporation 
of multimodal exercise features (e.g., drawings and formulas) (Yin et al., 
2019) to capture more exercise information for comprehensive exercise 
representations. It is a feasible approach to explore more reasonable 
methods of constructing exercise heterogeneous graphs, incorporating 
the domain knowledge of exercises, to aggregate multimodal informa­
tion and obtain more accurate exercise representations. Third, we 
acknowledge the significance of a spatiotemporal approach, which can 
be influenced by multiple factors when capturing the temporal de­
pendencies and spatial correlations within the data. For example, 
depending on the interaction sequence, we can segment time series data 
into short-term, mid-term, and long-term subsequences, and utilize 
diverse techniques to extract crucial spatiotemporal features (Xu et al., 
2023), such as principal component analysis or causal analysis. Lastly, 
the applications of KT algorithms hold great promise in revolutionizing 
online education systems by offering more accurate diagnostic services 
for assessing student knowledge status. By dynamically tracing students’ 
cognitive levels, we can construct a learning dashboard (Aguilar et al., 
2021) or design scaffolding adaptive prompts (Ueno & Miyazawa, 
2017), to help students achieve high-quality personalized learning and 
facilitate differentiated teaching. We believe that these research di­
rections hold significant potential for further exploration in the future.
