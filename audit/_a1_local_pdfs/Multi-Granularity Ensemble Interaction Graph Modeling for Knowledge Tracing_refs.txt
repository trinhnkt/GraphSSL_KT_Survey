In summary, there are two key observations:
• By combining the results from fine-grained, mid-grained, and
overall levels of modeling, we can gain a more comprehensive
understanding of the student’s learning state and provide person-
alized learning suggestions accordingly.
• Considering the correlations of interactions between students is
significantly beneficial for accurately tracing and understanding
their knowledge states, which not only aids the model in deeply
uncovering the underlying connections within historical interac-
tion data but also effectively captures the dynamic changes in
a student’s knowledge state when answering exercises involving
potentially related knowledge concepts.
5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose a KT model named MGIGKT, which mod-
els students’ knowledge states from dynamic, time-based perspectives
and static, interaction-graph-based perspectives to predict their fu-
ture performance. We first finely partition students’ historical learning
records, modeling their knowledge states granularly in the Multi-Scale
Sequential Perception Modeling module to explore learning patterns
at multiple granularities. Simultaneously, we statically model students’
knowledge states from two constructed interaction views in the In-
teraction Graph Dependency Modeling module to capture the latent
correlations in student interactions comprehensively. MGIGKT captures
different types of contextual information in student learning records,
enhancing its modeling capability. Experimental validation on four
datasets confirms the effectiveness of our proposed model.
