A B S T R A C T
Knowledge tracing (KT) has evolved into a crucial component of the online education system with the
rapid development of online adaptive learning. A key component of the online education system, knowledge
tracing (KT) assesses the state of knowledge by tracing each learner’s learning activities. The deep KT model,
however, is unable to completely extract the features of the questions and skills due to the heterogeneity
of the knowledge structure and the sparsity of the interaction records. The model’s capacity to handle
diverse data is also restricted by over parameterization. Additionally, rather than focusing solely on a
precise fit, Intelligent Tutoring System (ITS) should stress interpretable feedback to the learner. The deep
KT approach’s item parameters are still unable to give students useful feedback. This paper proposes to
trace learner’s short-term attentional knowledge based on heterogeneous hierarchical differentiation, named
HHSKT. Hierarchical heterogeneous knowledge structures and short-term memory enhancement will be used to
model the effects of different interaction sequences on learners. Specifically, knowledge structure features are
extracted by constructing a heterogeneous graph-based graph information augmentation component. Question
differentiation parameters are derived by transforming the TrueSkill system. Besides, learners’ history-related
practices are emphasized by windowing attention. Comparing regression-based and deep-based knowledge
tracing experiments shows that HHSKT significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art approach on three real-
world benchmark datasets (with an average AUC improvement of up to 3%), demonstrating the superiority of
the proposed model.
1. Introduction
In recent years, online schooling on a massive scale has exploded,
especially following the pandemic. Simultaneously, homogeneous on-
line learning content cannot satisfy the individualized learning needs
of a growing number of users (Injadat, Moubayed, Nassif, & Shami,
2020; Lan & Baraniuk, 2016). Current online learning platforms lack
the adaptability to address these difficulties and provide learners with
more specific, individualized feedback and help. The COVID-19-related
school closures pushed schools to move instruction online and pro-
vide remote support for students (Boltz, Yadav, Dillman, & Robertson,
2021). Therefore, the traditional method of measuring the knowledge
of learners’ based on test scores and written responses lacks the nec-
essary tools and environments to promote high-quality and thoughtful
remote learning.

Data analysis and intelligent tutoring systems rely heavily on edu-
cational data mining (de Andrade, Rigo, & Barbosa, 2021). Knowledge
Tracing (KT) is a learning situation analysis task based on computer-
assisted large-scale data processing capabilities to trace learning activ-
ities, which can deliver diagnostic results without sophisticated human
engagement for learners and teachers (Minn, Vie, Takeuchi, Kashima, &
Zhu, 2021). Knowledge components (KCs) constitute learning content,
and a learner’s knowledge states (KS) are a collection of KCs. As
demonstrated in Fig. 1, a learner’s performance on the eleventh exercise
can be estimated by tracing their mastery of KCs on the first ten
exercises.
There are several approaches in the KT field that may be used to
measure a learner’s mastery of KCs, including bayesian methods (Zhao,

Bhatt, Thille, Zimmaro and Gattani, 2020), logistic regression meth-
ods (Vie & Kashima, 2019), and deep learning methods (Piech et al.,
2015). Psychometric approaches based on psychology (based on statis-
tical regression, etc.) and Bayesian-based methods are typical examples
of traditional methods (Ha, Hong, Hwang, & Yoon, 2018). Item re-

et al., 2021), where Rasch (1PL-IRT) is also employed to estimate
item difficulty (Ghosh, Heffernan, & Lan, 2020). Some recurrent neural

dependencies between interactions (Ding & Larson, 2020; Lin & Chi,

proposed to emphasize the weights of different KCs (Oya & Morishima,
2021; Pandey & Karypis, 2019; Pu, Yudelson, Ou, & Huang, 2020).
These deep models have demonstrated impressive performance thanks
to their robust fitting capabilities.
Deep knowledge tracing (DKT) is an early RNN-based deep KT
model (Piech et al., 2015), while self-attentive knowledge tracing
(SAKT) is the first model to integrate the transformer structure into
KT (Pandey & Karypis, 2019). The RNN structure may step-by-step
process sequential input from left to right and from bottom to top,
retaining specific information of memory units depending on gating
information and retaining the information recorded during steps 𝑡0 to
𝑡𝑛−1 to forecast 𝑡𝑛(Graves, rahman Mohamed, & Hinton, 2013). Online
learning data consist mostly of sequential information, which is ideally
suited for processing by RNN architectures. SAKT proposes an attention
mechanism based on the sequential task in order to highlight the con-
tribution of various KCs to the prediction of KC mastery at the present
time. In addition to aforementioned models, graph neural networks
(GNNs) have garnered significant attention (Veliković et al., 2017),
and many graph construction methods have been proposed for models
such as graph-based interaction knowledge tracing (GIKT) (Yang et al.,
2021). Furthermore, the aggregation and representation capabilities
of graph networks have been applied to significantly improve the
prediction capability and interpretability of KT models substantially.

techniques now in use, including some cutting-edge technologies.
Despite the fact that, current deep KT models are superior to tradi-
tional models for predicting learners’ mastery of KC, the existing models
have three major flaws. First, the relationships between items, skills,
and learners in knowledge tracing datasets are often quite complex. In-
sufficient research exists on combining diverse features and large-scale
learner interaction network architectures. Secondly, interpretable item
parameters are frequently absent from deep model. It is more important
for ITS to provide learners with practical and solvable feedback than
model fitting capabilities. Lastly, DKT’s modeling of learners’ memory
skills relies solely on gated information, which is insufficiently inter-
pretable. The transformer-based approach can assign various weights to
sequential characteristics. Attention has visual explanatory advantages,
but nevertheless resists the memory process of the learner.
This work proposes HHSKT, a heterogeneous GNN-based model for
multi-level summarization of knowledge structures and learner atten-
tion reinforcement. Higher-order relationships between items and skills
are extracted by heterogeneous GNN, a rich representation of skill-level
information is obtained by heterogeneous graph coarsening, and the
final prediction is completed by item differentiation in conjunction with
the attention mechanism of window sequence memory.
The following is a summary of our key contributions in this pa-
per: (1) The model implements a higher-order item skill embedding
representation of heterogeneous learner interaction graphs based on
massive amounts of data. (2) The model transforms the TrueSkill
system to obtain interpretable parameters. Explainable item differences
and item difficulty are generated by generating learners with explain-
able feedback. (3) The model’s windowed attention is superior to the
mechanism’s original implementation. Learners’ short-term learning
status can be appropriately highlighted by implementing a sequential
positional attention method with windowed memory constraints.

2. Related work
This section provides a brief review of deep knowledge tracing, and
related work consists of three main parts: the application of GNNs in
the field of knowledge tracing, the TrueSkill system, and the study of
learner memory mechanisms.
2.1. Knowledge tracing
Currently, the great majority of prevalent KT models are currently
deep models, and this work focuses on deep knowledge tracing models
as well. Knowledge tracing has traditionally focused on predicting
the future performance of the learner and producing an evaluation
of the learner’s knowledge state (KS). Earlier knowledge tracing tech-
niques, such as Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT), depended on Hid-
den Markov Models (Zhao, Bhatt et al., 2020), Hidden Markov Models
can forecast the probability distribution of hidden variables based on
the present state of knowledge state (Hanghang, Xiaopeng, & Yuntao,
2021). In addition, learning factor analysis approaches (LFA) are es-
sential knowledge tracing techniques (Yu, 2018; Zhang et al., 2021).
There are several models in cognitive and psychometric factor analysis
methodologies, such as IRT, additive factor model (AFM), performance
factors analysis (PFA), etc. (Cen et al., 2006; Liao et al., 2012; Pavlik
et al., 2009). Modeling and tracking learning behaviors by investigating
factors related to learning capacities is referred to as factor analysis.
The suggested knowledge tracing machine (KTM) framework combines
IRT, AFM, and PFA models (Vie & Kashima, 2019). To predict the
likelihood of learners’ answers, KTMs utilize sparse sets of weights for
each attribute. The classic LFA approach can may alter the investigated
factors and so trace the knowledge states (KS) with a degree of flexibil-
ity, but is largely ineffective for long-interval tracing. The DASH and
DAS3H bridge the gap between factor analysis models and memory
models (Choffin et al., 2019; Lindsey et al., 2014). Knowledge tracing
