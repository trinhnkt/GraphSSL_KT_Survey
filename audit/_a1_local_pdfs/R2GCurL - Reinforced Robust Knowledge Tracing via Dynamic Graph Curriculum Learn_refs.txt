9
Conclusions and Future Work
R2GCurL unifies dynamic graph modeling and adaptive CL to improve personalized structural
modeling, robustness to noisy interactions, and training efficiency in KT. By representing student
trajectories as dynamic exercise–concept graphs, the framework captures both local interaction
structure and cumulative historical structure. The curriculum module further derives a continuity-
based structural signal from dynamic graph entropy and uses an RL-based scheduler to adapt
curriculum emphasis according to model feedback. Across five real-world datasets and multiple
noise settings, the proposed method consistently improves predictive performance while keeping
computational overhead within a practical range. The empirical results further show that the RL-
based scheduler provides clear advantages over simpler heuristic alternatives in noisy settings, and
that the resulting knowledge-state trajectories are more coherent and response-aligned. In future
work, we plan to validate these mechanisms in broader tutoring scenarios, extend the framework
to multimodal educational data, and investigate lighter-weight adaptive schedulers together with
richer trajectory-level interpretability analyses.
