A B S T R A C T
Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates students’ mastery of knowledge concepts or skills by analyzing their historical
interactions. Although general KT methods have effectively assessed students’ knowledge states, specific
measurements of students’ programming skills remain insufficient. Existing studies mainly rely on exercise
outcomes and do not fully utilize behavioral data during the programming process. Therefore, we integrate
a Logical and Grammar Skills Knowledge Tracing (LGS-KT) model to enhance programming education. This
model integrates static analysis and dynamic monitoring (such as CPU and memory consumption) to evaluate
code elements, providing a thorough assessment of code quality. By analyzing students’ multiple iterations
on the same programming problem, we constructed a reweighted logical skill evolution graph to assess the
development of students’ logical skills. Additionally, to enhance the interactions among representations with
similar grammatical skills, we developed a grammatical skills interaction graph based on the similarity of
knowledge concepts. This approach significantly improves the accuracy of inferring students’ programming
grammatical skill states. The LGS-KT model has demonstrated superior performance in predicting student
outcomes. Our research highlights the potential application of a KT model that integrates logical and
grammatical skills in programming exercises. To support reproducible research, we have published the data
and code at https://github.com/xinjiesun-ustc/LGS-KT, encouraging further innovation in this field.
1. Introduction
Knowledge tracing (KT) refers to the dynamic estimation of stu-
dents’ mastery of knowledge concepts (KCs) or skills (such as loops)
based on their responses to problems. These estimations are then used
to predict their future performance (Pandey & Karypis, 2019; Shen
et al., 2024), as shown in Fig. 1. The importance of programming skills
is becoming increasingly prominent, and more people are encouraged
to engage in programming exercises to enhance their individual pro-
gramming abilities (Lindberg, Laine, & Haaranen, 2019; Messer, Brown,
Kölling, & Shi, 2024). In this process, tracking learners’ programming
skills becomes crucial (Ashurova & Ashurov, 2023; Huang, Brusilovsky,
Guerra, Koedinger, & Schunn, 2023; Li et al., 2022).
In recent years, deep learning knowledge tracing (DLKT) mod-
els have flourished, encompassing autoregressive deep sequential KT
models (Chen, Liu, Huang, Liu, & Luo, 2023; Minn, Yu, Desmarais,
Zhu, & Vie, 2018; Wang et al., 2023), attention mechanisms (Ghosh,

Recently, programming KT tasks have garnered significant attention
from researchers. To enhance the ability to predict students’ program-
ming behaviors and knowledge states (Li et al., 2022; Liu, Wang,
Baraniuk, & Lan, 2022), various methods have been proposed. For in-
stance, Code-DKT (Shi, Chi, Barnes, & Price, 2022) integrates Code2Vec
with the DKT model (Piech et al., 2015) to embed student code and
model knowledge states; the PTDK model (Zhu et al., 2022), on the
other hand, employs dual-sequence modeling techniques to accurately
predict the outcomes of students’ subsequent submissions.
Although DLKT methods have introduced new perspectives for han-
dling general KT issues, the programming exercise process (PEP) sig-
nificantly differs from general exercise processes. In general exercises,
learners answer questions in sequence, and each question is not at-
tempted multiple times in a row, as shown in Fig. 1(a). In contrast, PEP

allows learners to solve problems through an iterative process. In each
exercise, learners can submit code, receive feedback, and repeatedly
modify their solutions, as depicted in Fig. 1(b). Unlike general exercises
(such as multiple-choice math questions), a learner’s proficiency is not
only reflected in whether they answer questions correctly but is also
directly manifested in the programming skills embodied in the code
they submit. This includes the relevance of grammatical skills used
across different programming questions and the evolution of students’
programming logical skills during the continuous code iteration process
within the same question. This process generates richer behavioral
data for each learner than general exercises, requiring more careful
consideration when assessing programming skills proficiency.
To ensure accurate measurement of skill proficiency in program-
ming education, we must address three major challenges. Firstly, it is
crucial to perform detailed information extraction, grammatical analy-
sis, and code quality assessment on students’ submitted code. Although
students’ code harbors rich information about programming skills, ex-
isting code embedding technologies (Devlin, Chang, Lee, & Toutanova,
2018; Feng et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2020; Hinton & Salakhutdinov,
2006) primarily focus on the structure and semantics of the source
code, often failing to delve into data directly related to programming
skills. Secondly, given that the same problem in programming ex-
ercises often involves continuous code iteration aimed at enhancing
programming logic, effectively assessing the evolution of students’
problem-solving approaches and the growth of their programming log-
ical skills from these continuous changes poses a significant challenge.
Lastly, uncovering the grammatical knowledge similarities between
different problems and building interactive representations of gram-
matical skills based on these similarities are key to enhancing the
accuracy and practicality of the model. To address these issues, we need
to adopt a comprehensive approach that not only considers the person-
alized characteristics of students’ programming logical skills but also
deeply understands their mastery of grammatical knowledge, to achieve
a more objective and precise assessment of students’ programming skill
states.
To effectively address the challenges mentioned above, we propose
the logical and Grammatical Skills KT (LGS-KT) model, which inno-
vates in several key aspects aimed at enhancing the effectiveness and
interpretability of KT in programming exercises. Firstly, to deeply mine
the information contained in the code, we use static analysis combined
with dynamic monitoring (such as CPU and memory consumption) to
assess code quality and score different programming grammar elements
within the code. Secondly, to evaluate the development of students’
logical skills during the programming process, we perform differential
analysis on multiple submissions of the same problem and, combining
code quality, grammar scoring, and source code information, we con-
struct a reweighted logical skills evolution graph. Lastly, to facilitate
interaction among representations with similar grammatical skills, we
first establish a similarity graph of knowledge concepts and then, based
on this graph, extract the grammatical knowledge relationships in-
volved by different students to build a grammar skill interaction graph,
enhancing the model’s ability to infer students’ programming grammat-
ical skill states. These innovations together significantly improve the
model’s practicality and explanatory power.
In this study, we propose a new model for KT in programming
exercises, aimed at more accurately and comprehensively assessing stu-
dents’ programming skills. Our main contributions can be summarized
as follows:
• We propose an innovative assessment method that integrates
logical and grammatical skills in programming exercises to more
precisely evaluate students’ proficiency in programming skills.
• By constructing a reweighted logical Skills evolution graph and a
grammatical skills interaction graph, our model can meticulously
track and analyze changes and developments in students’ skills
during the programming exercise. The introduction of these stu-
dent profiles provides new perspectives and deep insights into
how students gradually master complex programming tasks.
• In experiments comparing our LGS-KT model with existing KT
models, our model demonstrated superior performance across
multiple datasets. This result confirms the effectiveness and ed-
ucational value of our model in practical applications.
These contributions not only showcase the innovation of our re-
search but also provide powerful tools for programming education
practice, helping educators better understand and support students’
learning processes.
2. Related works
Accurately identifying students’ programming skills during coding
exercises is an extremely valuable task. Subsequently, we will explore
relevant research from two perspectives: KT and modeling in computer
science education.
2.1. Knowledge tracing
Recently, deep learning technologies have been extensively applied
to KT tasks, which model students’ historical learning patterns and
predict future performance. Existing DLKT methods can be categorized
into two main types based on their application scenarios: academic and
programming subjects.
C1: Academic Subjects Modeling the knowledge states of students
in academic subjects is one of the most important applications of KT
tasks. These studies not only enhance the performance of the models
but also provide us with new perspectives and tools to deeply un-
derstand students’ learning processes. In recent years, the application
of deep learning technologies in the field of KT has continuously
driven research progress. Researchers are dedicated to improving the
foundational structures of models to more accurately trace students’
knowledge states. By incorporating Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
layers (Piech et al., 2015), memory networks (Abdelrahman & Wang,
2019; Sun et al., 2021; Zhang, Shi, King, & Yeung, 2017), and at-
tention mechanisms (Ghosh et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2024; Pandey
& Karypis, 2019), these methods can better differentiate students’
mastery of various KCs and improve the accuracy of the models. In
addition to improvements in model structure, researchers have also
begun to incorporate problem-solving features to gather more com-
prehensive information and more accurately understand and predict
