A B S T R A C T
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a fundamental and challenging task in intelligent education, aiming to trace 
learners’ knowledge states and learning processes, providing better support and guidance for teaching and 
addressing mental factors. Previous KT tasks have focused on considering learners’ exposure to extrinsic 
environmental factors while ignoring the influence of intrinsic psychological factors. Moreover, previous 
methods have adopted a single perspective in modeling learners’ knowledge states, ignoring the diversity 
of states in the learning process. To address these issues, we define the concept of cognitive situation through 
the guidance of cognitive psychology theory to help to explain the extrinsic influence and intrinsic cognition 
of learners within complex learning environments. Moreover, we design a Cognitive Situation-based Graph 
KT (CSGKT) model to quantify learners’ influences in the cognitive process by modeling schemas capturing 
intrinsic characteristics and extrinsic factors through Hyper-Graph Neural Networks (HGNN). Second, we utilize 
a Directed Graph Convolutional Neural Network (DGCNN) to capture the correlation information between 
knowledge concepts and structure the learner’s cognitive activities and knowledge states, adding a detailed 
representation of multiple states of the learning process. In addition, we use the Erase-add Gate to filter out 
the knowledge states that do not match the learner’s current cognitive activities to stabilize the learner’s 
due cognition. In our experiments, we selected nine baseline models from three mainstream approaches, 
including sequence-based approaches, Transformer-based approaches, and complex structure-based approaches. 
The experimental results show that our models outperform these baseline models. At the same time, we also 
verify two classic assertions in cognitive psychology, namely, the ‘‘short-term memory forgetting of knowledge 
concepts is mainly caused by interference rather than memory trace fading" and the ‘‘cognitive imagery and 
perceptual function play an equivalent role in the cognitive process", which further support the feasibility of 
the model.
1. Introduction
The rapid development of artificial intelligence and big data tech-
nologies has made intelligent education a key innovation in the ed-
ucational field. Technologies [1] such as Knowledge Tracing (KT) 
provide personalized learning [2,3] support through real-time analysis 
of learners’ behaviors and knowledge states, aiming to improve learning 
efficiency and educational quality by optimizing resource allocation. 
However, a major challenge in achieving intelligent education is accu-
rately understanding learners’ cognitive processes in complex learning 
environments. Learning is a social process embedded in specific con-
texts, influenced by external factors and individual characteristics. 
Ignoring this complexity hinders a comprehensive understanding of 
learners’ cognitive states and impairs personalized support.

Education is inherently a complex system [4], encompassing nu-
merous intricate factors such as interactions between individuals and 
groups, mutual shaping of environment and cognition, and the inte-
gration and analysis of multi-source heterogeneous data. A holistic 
understanding of learning environments necessitates interdisciplinary 
theoretical exploration and methodological innovation. Cognitive psy-
chology provides a crucial theoretical foundation for comprehending 
cognitive processes in learning. According to cognitive psychologist 
Beck [5], human cognitive activities invariably unfold within specific 
situations, with situational factors such as learning environment, pre-
sentation of learning materials, and types of learning tasks influencing 
cognitive processing, including attention, memory, and thinking. In 
other words, learners are embodied, contextualized cognitive subjects.

In recent years, the field of cognitive psychology has witnessed a 
proliferation of studies exploring cognitive engagement and cognitive 
states, offering new perspectives for understanding cognitive dynamics 
in learning processes. Liu et al. [6] employed deep learning methods to 
identify learners’ cognitive states, revealing e-learning patterns under 
different cognitive engagement modes. Some studies focus on analyzing 
the associations between cognitive factors and learning outcomes, such 
as predicting academic performance based on emotions and cogni-
tive engagement [7], and the impact of cognitive interference and 
emotional patterns on academic achievement [8]. Additionally, some 
scholars have proposed novel approaches like semi-supervised learn-
ing for identifying cognitive patterns in online learning discussions, 
achieving favorable results [9]. These studies expand the application 
of cognitive modeling in intelligent education; however, they still lack 
a systematic consideration of the multi-dimensional influencing factors 
in complex learning environments.
To overcome the limitations of existing KT methods, this paper 
introduces the concept of cognitive situation. A cognitive situation specif-
ically refers to a learner’s cognitive processing in a particular spatiotem-
poral situation, emphasizing the dynamic associations among extrinsic 
factors, intrinsic characteristics, cognitive activities, and knowledge 
states, thereby establishing a contextualized learner cognitive model. 
This concept provides a theoretical framework for analyzing key ele-
ments and their interaction patterns in learning environments. Within 
a cognitive situation, it is posited that a learner’s knowledge state not 
only updates but also be influenced by intrinsic characteristics such as 
individual differences, perceptions of task difficulty, knowledge recep-
tion capacity, and adaptation to teaching methods, leading to variations 
in knowledge states. Fig. 1 illustrates that at each time step, both 
knowledge states and cognitive activities change to varying degrees due 
to the influence of extrinsic factors and intrinsic characteristics. By fo-
cusing on cognitive situations, a more comprehensive understanding of 
learners’ cognitive change patterns in complex learning environments 
can be achieved. This approach supports the design of personalized 
and effective learning strategies, thereby enhancing learners’ learning 
outcomes and mastery levels.
Our specific contributions are described as follows:
• By leveraging insights from cognitive psychology and typical 
application scenarios, we propose defining the cognitive situation 
and developing a correlation analysis model based on ‘‘extrin-
sic factors-cognitive process-intrinsic characteristics’’. This model 
aims to enhance the alignment of KT tasks with real-world situa-
tions.
• CSGKT captures the schema of intrinsic features and extrinsic 
factors through HGNN modeling, and DGCNN is modeled to 
capture the correlation information between knowledge concepts 
and Erase-add Gate to rationalize learners’ cognition.
• Extensive experiments were conducted on four benchmark datasets 
and compared with nine baseline models from three mainstream 
approaches. The experimental results show that CSGKT achieves 
the most advanced prediction performance.
• Two major experiments in cognitive psychology were simulated 
to validate the two classic assertions: ‘‘short-term memory for-
getting of knowledge concepts is mainly caused by interference 
rather than memory trace fading’’ and ‘‘cognitive imagery and 
perceptual functions play an equivalent role in the cognitive 
process’’.

2. Related work
In this section, we begin with an extensive review of the three 
dominant approaches. We then outline the specific definitions that give 
cognitive situations, as they are closely related to the work in this 
paper.
2.1. Knowledge tracing
KT [10,11] as a fundamental task in intelligent education, possesses 
a wide range of potential and has come to prominence in recent 
decades. It involves continuous monitoring, assessment of learners’ 
knowledge and skills, and tracing of the knowledge state. This tracing 
not only helps to understand the individual needs and development 
of learners but can also be used to adjust educational strategies to 
better meet the learning needs of learners. When it comes to the 
high performance and timeliness of KT research, it can usually be 
categorized into three main types of approaches: sequence-based ap-
proaches, Transformer-based approaches, and complex structure-based 
approaches. Each of these approaches has different advantages and 
applicability, and the appropriate model can be selected to improve 
the performance and efficiency of KT based on specific research and 
educational scenarios.
