Weighted Heterogeneous Graph-Based Three-View
Contrastive Learning for Knowledge Tracing
in Personalized e-Learning Systems
Jianwen Sun
, Shangheng Du
, Zhi Liu
, Fenghua Yu, Sannyuya Liu
, and Xiaoxuan Shen
Abstract—Personalized e-learning systems are applications of
consumer electronics in the ﬁeld of education that provide indi-
vidualized and adaptive services for users. Knowledge tracing
(KT), as a key technology, aims to model learners’ knowledge
states through the interactions between learners and learning
resources, and predicts their future performance. However, the
problem of interaction sparsity in educational resources leads
to the fact that simple representations of questions usually
fail to accurately capture students’ knowledge states. In this
paper, inspired by the data-driven paradigm, we propose a
novel knowledge tracing method named weighted heterogeneous
graph-based Three-view Contrastive Learning framework for
Knowledge Tracing (TCL4KT). Technically, three different view
encoders in TCL4KT complement each other to obtain ques-
tion embeddings with rich information. Speciﬁcally, TCL4KT
considers the semantic information of higher-order, heteroge-
neous and the downstream task on a weighted heterogeneous
graph of KT to learn high-quality representations. Besides, a
meta-path-based positive sample selection strategy and joint con-
trastive loss are employed to gain better prediction performance.
Experimental results on four datasets demonstrate the superi-
ority of TCL4KT over baseline models, and further analysis
veriﬁes the effectiveness of our three-view contrastive learning
framework.

Knowledge tracing (KT), as a modeling technology for learn-
ers, has become an important research content in this ﬁeld [5].
KT, shown in Fig. 1, aims to estimate the current mastery
states of learners through their historical learning records so
as to predict the future performance, provide more targeted
guidance, or make more personalized recommendations [6].
The existing KT models can be categorized into the tra-
ditional KT models [6], [7] and deep learning-based KT
models [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]. In most
early KT models [6], [8], [9], researchers only utilized skill
information to represent each question and evaluate students’
mastery of skills. These skill-level KT models often overlook
the rich information contained in the questions themselves,
such as difﬁculty and discrimination, resulting in an inability to
distinguish different questions with the same skill. Therefore,
in some follow-up related approaches, researchers learn the
representations by incorporating various kinds of information
from the questions [16], [17]. However, due to the excessive
number of questions compared to the number of skills, there
is a sparsity issue in the interaction between students and
questions (i.e., many questions have few records of attempts),
which ultimately limits the performance of the model [18].

tive in solving various complex problems, including mining

dancy [22], etc. Consequently, researchers began to introduce
graph structure to alleviate the problem of data sparsity by
mining the corresponding relations between questions and
skills (or other entities) on graphs [23], [24], [25], [26], [27].
In these graph structures, multiple questions are often asso-
ciated with a single skill, while a question can belong to
multiple skills simultaneously. These relations on graphs cap-
ture the similarity information of questions (e.g., the questions
with the same skills may be similar), allowing the model to
leverage the interaction history of similar questions and make
more accurate predictions for the current question. Although
these KT methods using graph structure have achieved some
results, most of them only consider relations between ques-
tions and skills, and ignore the interaction between students
and questions. Since the core of KT is to solve human-related
problems, it is necessary to consider the information of learn-
ers in representations, so our paper constructs a heterogeneous
graph containing three entities: questions, skills, and students.
Different from other KT studies that introduce student enti-
ties [28], [29], due to the different types of three entities and
inconsistent interaction modes, we expand the heterogeneous
graph to a weighted heterogeneous graph, and employ the
contrastive learning method to mine the weight information
on different edges to capture higher-order semantics between
questions.
Self-supervised learning technology [30] uses pretext tasks
to extract supervision information from large-scale unsuper-
vised data, and trains the network through the constructed
supervision information, so as to learn valuable representations
for downstream tasks. Recently, the contrastive learning (CL)
method under the self-supervised framework has also been
applied to the ﬁeld of KT [31], [32], [33], optimizing KT tasks
through joint comparative loss functions. However, these meth-
ods are only simply combined the traditional self-supervision
framework and KT, and their contrastive mechanisms often
ignore the characteristics of KT task itself (e.g., the hetero-
geneity of KT scenario, the relations between questions and
skills, and the similarity of students, etc.), and even less can
they consider the different relations of heterogeneous graph of
KT in various perspectives.
In our paper, we propose a weighted heterogeneous
graph-based three-view contrastive learning framework for
knowledge tracing (TCL4KT), which exploits and integrates
information of three views (meta-path, network schema
and knowledge tracing) to gain question representations.
Then, a special positive sample selection strategy and con-
trastive mechanisms are applied to maximize the mutual
information between different views of the same question.
Finally, we combine the encoded question embeddings and
answer records to predict the future performance of stu-
dents through a recurrent neural network (RNN). Compared
with other baseline models, our method shows the best
prediction accuracy and generalization ability on 4 com-
mon datasets. The effectiveness of our three-view contrastive
learning framework is also veriﬁed through a series of
analytical experiments. Our contributions are summarized
as follows:
• To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst time
to propose a weighted heterogeneous graph-based CL
framework in KT ﬁeld which use three complementary
view encoders integrating multiple relational information
to learn more high-quality question embeddings for the
prediction task.
• For GCL, we break the tradition of separating the CL
framework from the downstream task during training,
and propose a novel heterogeneous graph-based three-
view contrastive learning framework, which uses network
schema view and downstream task view to assist the
meta-path view to learn node embedding, and a positive
sample selection strategy and contrastive mechanism are
designed to achieve better application and generalization
capabilities for downstream tasks.
• We perform experiments on 4 public datasets, and the
results show that the prediction performance of TC4KT
is signiﬁcantly improved. In addition, ablation researches
verify the effectiveness of each module.

Section IV gives the details and results of the experiment, and

II. RELATED WORKS
In this part, we introduce technologies of KT including tra-
ditional KT, deep KT and the recent popular graph-based KT,
and the related works of graph contrastive learning.
A. Knowledge Tracing
There are two kinds of existing knowledge tracing meth-
ods: traditional machine learning methods and deep learning
methods. This paper mainly studies on the deep KT methods.
In 1994, Corbett and Anderson [6] ﬁrst applied knowledge
tracing technology into the ﬁeld of intelligent education
and proposed Bayesian knowledge tracing based on hidden
Markov model.
With the development of deep learning technology, deep
learning-based KT models [8], [9], [11] have shown supe-
rior performance over traditional machine learning models [6].
Song et al. [34] categorize various deep KT models into DKT
and its variants, memory network-based models, attention
mechanism-based models, and graph-based models based on
speciﬁc technologies. Piech et al. [8] employs deep learning to
KT and proposes Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT). Dynamic
Key-Value Memory Networks (DKVMN) [9] utilizes a static
key matrix and a dynamic value matrix to store latent knowl-
edge skills and update the mastery of corresponding skills.
Self-Attentive Knowledge Tracing (SAKT) [11] applies the
self-attention mechanism [35] in KT to solve the problem of
sparsity in learning interactions. Recently, the potential graph
structure of KT has been explored to mine rich information,
which mainly achieved by the question-skill relation to con-
struct bipartite graphs or heterogeneous graphs [23], [24], [25],
[26], [27]. In [36], a heterogeneous graph with difﬁculty, ques-
