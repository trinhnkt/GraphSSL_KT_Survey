A B S T R A C T
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is an important research area in online education that focuses on
predicting future academic performance based on students’ historical exercise records. The key
to solving the KT problem lies in assessing students’ knowledge states through their responses
to concept-related exercises. However, analyzing exercise records from a single perspective
does not provide a comprehensive model of student knowledge. The truth is that students’
knowledge states often exhibit long- and short-term phenomena, corresponding to long-term
knowledge systems and short-term real-time learning, both of which are closely related to
learning quality and preferences. Existing studies have often neglected the learning preferences
implied by long-term knowledge states and their impact on student performance. Therefore, we
introduce a hybrid knowledge tracing model that utilizes both long- and short-term knowledge
state representations (L-SKSKT). It enhances KT by fusing these two types of knowledge state
representations and measuring their impact on learning quality. L-SKSKT includes a graph
construction method designed to model students’ long- and short-term knowledge states. In
addition, L-SKSKT incorporates a knowledge state graph embedding model that can effectively
capture long- and short-term dependencies, generating corresponding knowledge state repre-
sentations. Furthermore, we propose a fusion mechanism to integrate these representations and
trace their impact on learning outcomes. Extensive empirical results on four benchmark datasets
show that our approach achieves the best performance for KT, and beats various strong baselines
with a large margin.

traditional teaching methods. These platforms offer significant advantages, such as flexibility, convenience, rich resources, and
diverse curriculum options, making them highly attractive in modern education (Cole et al., 2017; Liu, Huang et al., 2023;
Zhang et al., 2025). Meanwhile, the capability of platforms to automatically monitor students’ learning progress and knowledge

KT can help teachers to dynamically adjust teaching schedules, learning materials and provide students with learning services that
support personalized and adaptive learning in online learning systems (Wang, Wu et al., 2019). Since KT tasks are too abstract and
difficult to define accurately, researchers often convert it into prediction tasks based on students’ knowledge state representations.
In the literature, previous KT models have evolved from traditional approaches such as probabilistic models (Baker et al., 2008) to
deep learning-based approaches (Gan et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2020). Deep learning-based approaches are based on deep neural
networks such as deep knowledge tracing (DKT) using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) (Piech et al., 2015), dynamic key–value
memory networks (DKVMN) using memory networks (MNs) (Zhang, Shi et al., 2017) and graph knowledge tracing (GKT) using
graph neural networks (GNNs) (Nakagawa et al., 2019). Among them, GNN-based KT exhibits superior performance because they
model concepts or exercises as nodes in a unified graph representation learning framework, which effectively simulates the structural
relationships between different concepts and the interactions between different exercises to model students’ knowledge states.
Although these existing approaches have made great advancements, they are still unsatisfactory. The main limitation of existing
research is that it focuses on constructing students’ knowledge state embedding based on the knowledge concepts and related
exercises they have learned in a short period of time, analyzes the short-term iterative process, ignoring the comprehensive
knowledge system constructed by the students in the long-term learning process, and lacking a more holistic analysis of the
knowledge states. Meanwhile, existing research on knowledge state modeling is relatively limited, which restricts its applicability and
flexibility in handling complex knowledge state embedding. In summary, the potential for comprehensive modeling of knowledge
states to improve KT performance remains to be explored.
In practice, we observe in conjunction with cognitive load theory (Krathwohl, 2002; Paas et al., 2016) that students generally
possess relatively stable long-term knowledge states and dynamic short-term knowledge states. Long-term knowledge states reflect
students’ deep understanding of knowledge and overall learning preferences, corresponding to the stable knowledge system. In
contrast, the short-term knowledge state reflects recent learning preferences and outcomes, usually shows the real-time change
phenomenon. This implies that accurately modeling a student’s knowledge state requires considering both long- and short-term
perspectives. The modeling approach should not only comprehensively assess the student’s overall learning process to capture their
long-term knowledge state, but also focus on their recent learning activities and performance to update their short-term knowledge
state in real-time. This method is crucial for improving the accuracy of KT prediction.
