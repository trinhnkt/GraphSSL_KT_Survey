ticular knowledge or concept based on the student’s historical learning interactions with

1. Introduction
As a cognitive model with the goal of mastery-based learning, Knowledge Tracing (KT) is ﬁrst introduced into the ﬁeld of
intelligent tutoring systems in [6]. It seeks to monitor the evolving state of students’ mastery of a speciﬁc learning concept or
knowledge when they interact with several corresponding exercises. The intent of this task is to personalise the sequence of
practice to enable efﬁcient mastery of the concepts, and to accurately predict student performance. With the rise of the inter-
net and online education, knowledge tracing is being used more and more widely on various educational platforms, and is
becoming a popular research topic.
Various approaches have been proposed to solve the KT problems. There are two most representative models: Bayesian
Knowledge Tracking (BKT) and Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT). BKT [6] models a student’s potential knowledge state as a set
of binary variables, each representing whether the student understands the corresponding concept.[26] applies RNNs [28] to
the ﬁeld of knowledge tracing, denoted as DKT. Compared to BKT, DKT takes the advantage of multiple free parameters and

other developed methods. Inspired by memory-enhanced neural networks, dynamic Key-Value Memory Networks (DKVMN)

tionship between exercises and concepts to predict the student’s mastery state of a certain concept, such models often fail to
make accurate prediction when a student is confronted with a concept that has not been previously covered, and are also
hard to capture the high-level semantic information.
To ﬁll in these gaps, we propose a Joint graph convolutional network based deep Knowledge Tracing (JKT) framework that
focuses on improving prediction accuracy as well as the interpretability of the method by exploring the implicit relationships
of ‘‘exercise-to-exercise” and ‘‘concept-to-concept”. As shown in Fig. 1, We ﬁrst take one student’s historical interactions as a
sample and count all the co-occurred exercise pairs that he answers correctly. We then consider a student to have mastered a
concept when he obtains an above-average accuracy rate for that concept. Similarly, we count all co-occurring pairs of con-
cepts that he has mastered and then calculate the ratio of the number of these co-occurring concepts that he has mastered to
the number of occurrences of these co-occurring concepts, indicating the co-mastery rate.
We can then establish the exercise inﬂuence sub-graph and the concept inﬂuence sub-graph based on the accumulated
co-correctness rate of each pair of co-occurred exercises and co-mastery rate of each pair of co-occurred concepts, respec-
tively. Given the established sub-graphs, we can learn the embeddings of each exercise and its corresponding concept
through a two-layer Graph convolutional network. Finally, we input these dense embeddings instead of traditional one-
hot or multi-hot vector representations into a RNN-based prediction layer to make prediction.
Compared to the existing methods, our proposed graph structure representation and the exploration of the relationships
of ‘‘exercise-to-exercise” and ‘‘concept-to-concept” allow for better and multi-dimensional establishment of connections
between exercises under different concepts. Furthermore, with powerful graph embeddings and graph convolution, we
can transform long and sparse one-hot or multi-hot vector representations into dense ones. At the same time, due to involv-
ing the co-correctness rate and co-mastery rate, the embeddings with higher co-correctness rate and co-mastery rate are
more likely to have higher similarity in the vector expressions. This feature can help to improve the prediction accuracy
as well as the interpretability of the method.
The contributions of our work are summarized as follows:
 As far as we know, this is the ﬁrst attempt to study the ‘‘exercise-to-exercise”, and ‘‘concept-to-concept” relationships in the
joint attributed KT graph structure. Our work can learn the reliable connections between exercises under different con-
cepts, further promoting the accuracy of student performance prediction and enhancing the interpretability of KT-based
applications.
 We propose a novel framework called Joint graph convolutional network deep Knowledge Tracing (JKT), which includes
both the exercise inﬂuence sub-graph and the concept inﬂuence sub-graph. Beneﬁting from this multidimensional sub-
graph fusion, the proposed JKT can take into account the importance of all ‘‘exercise-to-concept”, ‘‘exercise-to-exercise” and
‘‘concept-to-concept” relationships. Furthermore, another merit of JKT is able to capture the semantic information of stu-
dents’ learning states at higher levels in the knowledge tracing process.
 Sufﬁcient experiments and the corresponding ablation study on four real-world datasets have been conducted to demon-
strate that JKT perform better than other baseline models. More importantly, through an in-depth analysis of the
‘‘exercise-to-exercise”, and ‘‘concept-to-concept” relationships, the proposed JKT demonstrates its strong interpretability
in learning analysis.
