Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a fundamental task in intelligent education that tracks students’ knowledge
states through their historical interactions. Early deep learning-based KT mainly relies on sequential
models, which often find it more challenging to explicitly model the complex many-to-many relation-
ships between students and knowledge entities, such as exercises and knowledge components. Graph-
based Knowledge Tracing (GbKT) further leverages graph neural networks to explicitly represent these
structural relationships, creating new opportunities for learning-state monitoring and personalized inter-
vention. In this survey, we conduct an in-depth exploration of recent progress in GbKT research. First, we
introduce a two-dimensional taxonomy that aligns the primary modeling motivations with graph structural
forms, and we characterize them with unified message-passing equations. Second, focusing on applica-
tions, such as the discovery of knowledge structures, modeling with educational psychology theories and
interpretability, and personalized recommendation and path planning, we show how graph semantics en-
dow the models with new interpretability and intervention capabilities. Third, we systematically evaluate
17 public datasets, offering researchers guidance on dataset selection. Finally, we outline promising fu-
ture research directions for GbKT.
Keywords: knowledge tracing, graph-based knowledge tracing, graph neural networks, student model-
ing, educational data mining, personalized learning

the basis of their interaction records with previous exercises (Dowling and Hockemeyer, 2001;

Figure 1 illustrates a common approach in traditional KT: a student’s exercise–response se-

four exercises, e1–e4, at time steps t1–t4. Each exercise is linked to knowledge components (KC)

response sequence and the dependencies among KCs.
Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) (Corbett and Anderson, 1994) is the foundational model
in the knowledge tracing field. BKT is based on a Hidden Markov Model and uses four prob-
ability parameters—initial mastery probability, learning probability, guess probability, and slip
probability—to independently model each knowledge component. It updates the mastery proba-
bility estimate via Bayesian inference after each student response and has been widely deployed
in intelligent tutoring systems such as Cognitive Tutor and MATHia. Notably, BKT remains an
active research direction, with recent advances in interpretability (Zhou et al., 2024), parameter
theory (Shchepakin et al., 2024), and fairness (Barrett et al., 2024).
Meanwhile, the rise of Deep Learning Knowledge Tracing (DLKT) has opened new di-
rections for KT research. Compared to BKT’s assumption of independent modeling for each
KC, DLKT can capture more complex cross-skill learning dynamics and nonlinear temporal
dependencies. DKT (Piech et al., 2015) pioneered the application of recurrent neural networks
to KT; Dynamic Key-Value Memory Network (DKVMN) (Zhang et al., 2017) introduced dy-
namic key-value memory networks to directly output mastery levels for each concept. Subse-
quently, attention-based models further advanced the field: Self-Attentive KT (SAKT) (Pandey
and Karypis, 2019) and context-aware Attentive KT (AKT) (Ghosh et al., 2020) introduced self-
attention mechanisms to model the relevance of historical interactions and forgetting behavior;
