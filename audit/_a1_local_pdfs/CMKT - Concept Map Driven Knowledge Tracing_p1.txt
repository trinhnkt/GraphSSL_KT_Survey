CMKT: Concept Map Driven Knowledge Tracing
Yu Lu
, Penghe Chen
, Yang Pian
, and Vincent W. Zheng
Abstract—In this article, we advocate for and propose a novel
concept map driven knowledge tracing (CMKT) model, which
utilizes educational concept map for learner modeling. This article
particularly addresses the issue of learner data sparseness caused by
the unwillingness to practice and irregular learning behaviors on the
learner side. CMKT considers the concept map as a new information
source and explicitly exploits its inherent information to help the
estimation of the learner’s knowledge state. Speciﬁcally, the pairwise
educational relations in the concept map are formulated as the
ordering pairs and are used as mathematical constraints for model
construction. The topology information in the concept map is
extracted and used as the model input by employing the network
embedding
techniques.
Integrating
both
educational
relation
information and topology information in the concept map, CMKT
adopts the recurrent neural network to perform knowledge tracing
tasks.
Comprehensive
evaluations
conducted
on
ﬁve
public
educational datasets of four different subjects (more than 8000
learners and their 300 000 records) demonstrate the promise and
effectiveness of CMKT: The average area under ROC curve (AUC)
and overall prediction accuracy (ACC) achieve 0.82 and 0.75,
respectively, and CMKT outperforms all the baselines by at least
12.2% and 9.2% in terms of AUC and ACC.
Index Terms—Concept map, constraint learning, knowledge
tracing, learner modeling, network embedding.
I. INTRODUCTION
I
N the education realm, knowledge tracing (KT) refers to
modeling the knowledge states of learners through diag-
nosing their mastery level quantitatively on the individual
pedagogical concept (e.g., “fraction” or “linear equation” in
mathematics) [1]. The results of KT can be directly used to
estimate a learner’s current knowledge strength and weak-
ness, which has been serving a variety of intelligent tutoring
systems (ITS) and computer-supported education environ-
ments [2]. In addition, KT and its analytic results have been
used in today’s online learning platforms, including massive
open online courses (MOOCs) [3], to provide personalized
recommendations of learning resources and modules [4].
Traditionally, KT primarily uses learner’s exercise and
learning activity data to construct models and estimates a
learner’s knowledge state accordingly. The rapid development
of ITS and MOOCs provides good platforms for collecting
these data in a practical and inexpensive manner. However, it
also encounters a major challenge: the sparseness of learner’s
exercise and learning activity data. Speciﬁcally, learners tend
to use the online educational systems on an irregular time
basis and spend less time to practice or review individual con-
cepts as well as the comprehensive assessment [5]. For each
individual learner, it is likely that only a small set of concepts
in the target subject or course are practiced (i.e., by solving
quiz questions) or reviewed (i.e., by browsing tutorial videos
or interacting with tutors). As a result, such learners’ mastery
information most likely remains unknown on a large portion
of concepts, which makes KT models suffer from poor perfor-
mance and be restricted in other applications, including
learner academic performance prediction [6], question difﬁ-
culty estimation [7], and learning path adaptation [8].
However, it is impractical and unfair to impose any strict
learning or exercise routines on the learner side for collecting
necessary and adequate KT information. Other strategies for
handling data spareness, such as data augmentation [9] and
transfer learning [10], are also difﬁcult to be applied in KT due
to the complexity of the human’s cognitive process in the
knowledge domain. Thus, our insight into solving this data
sparseness problem hinges upon the information from the
knowledge domain itself. Speciﬁcally, we propose and advo-
cate the use of a concept map [11] by fully exploiting its inher-
ent information to help the KT model construction. Brieﬂy
speaking, a concept map refers to a graphic representation of
pedagogical concepts that illustrate different educational rela-
tions between concepts through linking connections. The well-
established knowledge space theory [12] in education reveals a
natural dependence between pedagogical concepts in the objec-
tive domain knowledge and subjective learner cognitive pro-
cess. In accordance with the knowledge space theory, human’s
learning process modeling could leverage the information
derived from the concept map. Fig. 1 illustrates a simpliﬁed
concept map that consists of six concepts (i.e., from C1 to C6)
with the prerequisite relations. Introducing concept map infor-
mation into knowledge state estimation provides a data source
for KT model construction and helps to mitigate the data
sparseness. In the education realm, particularly for K-12 educa-
tion, concept maps for most subjects and courses have been
constructed by domain experts or experienced teachers, which
can be directly used for either teaching or research purposes.

In general, two types of information from the concept map
can aid the knowledge state estimation and be used by KT
models: 1) educational relation information and 2) topology
information. The educational relations in concept maps pri-
marily specify the implicit but educationally signiﬁcant
dependencies between pedagogical concepts, which are com-
monly used to optimize the learning and teaching processes.
Take one type of educational relation called prerequisite rela-
tion as an example. Given some concepts serve as the prereq-
uisites of another concept (e.g., “adding” and “multiplication”
are the prerequisites of “factorization” in math), if a learner
has mastered the latter concept (i.e., “factorization”), it can be
inferred that the learner likely has mastered its prerequisite
ones (i.e., “adding” and “multiplication”). In addition to the
prerequisite relation, many other relation types, such as inclu-
sion, causal, and progressive relations, also appear in different
concept maps. On the other hand, the topology information
mainly refers to the physical structure of the concept map,
which indicates how each concept is linked with others and
how it is positioned in the entire map structure. For example, as
shown in Fig. 1, given a concept that occupies a central position
in a concept map (e.g., “adding” and “multiplication” serve
as the prerequisites for the concept “factorization,” and
“factorization” serves as the prerequisites for concepts “linear
equation” and “square root”), this concept (e.g., “factorization”)
would be more critical and important than other concepts that
are located at the map edges. Both the educational relation infor-
mation and the topology information from a concept map can be
integrated into KT models for better knowledge state estimation.
This article addresses these issues by introducing concept
map information into the KT model design. In particular, we
design a novel KT model that explicitly integrates the educa-
tional relation information and the topology information
derived from a concept map. There are several critical techni-
cal challenges in building the desired KT model. For example,
how to properly formulate the qualitative pairwise educational
relations as quantitative mathematical form and then integrate
with the KT model is not straightforward. Also, a concept
map naturally forms a directed acyclic graph (DAG), which
makes the common network embedding approaches targeting
generic graphs unsuitable [13]. Therefore, it is not a trivial
task to effectively extract the topology information from a
concept map and integrate it with the KT model. To solve
such challenges, we consider converting the pairwise educa-
tional relations to the speciﬁc mathematical constraints to reg-
ularize the learner state estimation. We also explore the new
embedding method to learn the topology information of a con-
cept map, which can be used as an additional input for the
new KT model.
Therefore, this article principally makes the following key
contributions.
1) New KT Model Design: To alleviate the issue of learner
data sparseness and better modeling learners, we pro-
pose a new KT model by leveraging on the inherent
information from a concept map.
2) Educational Relation Modeling: To properly use the
educational relations from a concept map, we model its
pairwise relations, typically the prerequisite ones, as the
ordering pairs, and then use them as mathematical con-
straints for the new KT model construction.
3) Topology Information Embedding: To directly use the
topology information from a concept map, we employ
network embedding techniques to extract the desired
information. The embedding results serve as the new
input for the proposed KT model.
4) Multidatasets and Comprehensive Evaluation: Using ﬁve
public educational datasets of four subjects (more than
8000 learners and their 300 000 records), we conduct
comprehensive experiments to evaluate the proposed
model, and the results show that the new KT model out-
performs all the baselines by at least 12.2% and 9.2% in
terms of average area under ROC curve (AUC) and over-
all prediction accuracy (ACC), respectively.
While the proposed new model is novel and effective, we
believe that the key impact of this article is to highlight a
broader possibility of learner modeling using the domain
knowledge and its inherent information, and eventually ena-
bles learners and their learning process to be better modeled,
analyzed, and predicted.
