A B S T R A C T
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a method that seeks to forecast students’ future performance based on their
historical interactions with intelligent tutoring systems. Various KT techniques have been developed from
distinct perspectives, such as probability, logic, and deep learning, to scrutinize interaction sequences.
However, most current methods predominantly concentrate on temporal or structural information, considering
simple question sequences or interaction sequences, but fail to describe the topological dependencies between
sequences, which can lead to changes in causal structures,as referred to as dynamic structural information in
the paper. The dynamic information includes the clustering effect among concepts and questions, as well
as the group effect among students and questions: Questions with the same knowledge concepts tend to
present increasingly similar response patterns in the sequence, and concurrently, students who answer similar
questions often exhibit closer knowledge states. We introduce the STHKT method, which employs a tripartite
heterogeneous graph to capture relationships among concepts, questions, and students. By incorporating
temporal information through a topological graph, we employ two Graph Convolution Networks (GCNs) to
model the two effects separately. Following this, we apply the Hawkes Process to introduce a spatiotemporal
attention mechanism that combines both effects. Through comparative experiments, ablation studies and case
studies, we validate the efficacy of key components in the model. Experiment results indicate that the STHKT
model can effectively model the two major effects, integrate dynamic structural information, and to some
extent improve the accuracy of predicting students’ response probabilities. It is the first time that these two
effects have been considered in the process of knowledge tracing, and it is adaptable to large-scale online
education platforms.
1. Introduction
Knowledge Tracing, as defined in Abdelrahman et al. (2023), Liu
et al. (2021), is a task aimed at modeling students’ knowledge profi-
ciency based on their learning interaction data. This is accomplished by
observing students’ responses to questions related to various concepts,
thereby indirectly tracing their knowledge states on specific concepts.
Generally speaking, most models simplify the learning environment by
presuming that students’ responses are binary (i.e., correct or incorrect).
Given a new question, the output of the knowledge tracing model is
the probability of students answering it correctly. This is one of the
most critical components in constructing adaptive education systems.

However, despite the notable success of the aforementioned meth-
ods, these approaches always focus on temporal or structural informa-
tion, considering simple question sequences or interaction sequences.
They often fail to adequately describe the topological dependencies
between these sequences, which can lead to changes in causal struc-
tures, namely dynamic structural information in the paper. This is
evident in two distinct effects: (1) the knowledge concept clustering
effect: questions sharing the same knowledge concepts tend to
present increasingly similar response patterns in the sequence.

We assert that it is vital and advantageous to contemplate the
concepts of clustering effect and student group effect within the context
of knowledge tracing. In this paper, we have proposed a Spatiotempo-
ral Knowledge Tracing framework (STHKT1) based on a Topological
Hawkes Process to better model the two effects mentioned above.
Firstly, to obtain the structural information in the knowledge tracing
process, we construct a tripartite heterogeneous graph comprising of
Concepts, Questions and Students (Su et al., 2022). Secondly, to com-
bine structural information with temporal information, we topologize
the question sequences and associated concepts and students within
the tripartite heterogeneous graph, thereby creating topological graphs.
During the topologization of a question sequence, we select question
nodes from the tripartite heterogeneous graph and generate directed
edges according to temporal relationships. Then, we identify all ad-
jacent concept and student nodes based on these question nodes. If
there is a common concept or student node between two question
nodes, the earlier one points to the shared node, followed by the later
question node, thereby forming a topological graph. Thirdly, to model
the influence of clustering effect and group effect with advantage, we
employ a graph convolutional method. This approach fundamentally
captures cumulative effects (Cai et al., 2022; Defferrard et al., 2016;
Shang & Sun, 2019). Ultimately, given that Hawkes processes and the
convolution process of topological graphs are essentially identical, we
combine the results of modeling the two effects with a monotonic
attention mechanism, using Hawkes process. It enhances the monotonic
attention to a spatiotemporal attention mechanism. By leveraging the
characteristics of topological graphs and Hawkes process, we integrate
temporal information into the knowledge tracing process, combining it
with the two effects to achieve higher prediction accuracy.
