Abstract
Knowledge Tracking (KT) predicts student performance by modeling the mastery of knowledge components in past
interactions. Although the latest research on KT is excellent to model behavior changes, the increasingly complex knowledge
components and semantic relationships of new exercises have made courses sparse and difficult to adequately diagnose
knowledge mastery. Hence, it is not enough to satisfy only the personalized learning. This paper proposes a graph-based
method with multi-hierarchical network (Caps-HAGKT) for tracking capsule knowledge, which integrates rich structural
information of the knowledge space, including multiple skills, prerequisites, attribution, and conceptual meta-paths. Firstly,
we use the multi-hierarchical knowledge capsule network to track a variety of high-level knowledge concepts, and then
extract the latent knowledge structure between levels. In addition, we adopt a new graph network with an external memory
matrix to model the relationship between concept capsules at the same level and update concept states. In previous works, it
was difficult to use complex graph information without any expert pre-definition. In Caps-HAGKT, the semantic attention of
knowledge capsules is used to merge different semantics in knowledge according to their importance. Extensive experiments
on some real-world datasets show that the prediction performance of Caps-HAGKT is better than that of the existing KT
method (AUC is increased by 3.05% on an average). The visualization of both latent graphs and case studies indicates the
excellent interpretability of predictions.

Knowledge tracking is an elemental research which can
effectively improve the participation rate and success rates
of educational institutions. It is widely used in online open
courses, intelligent tutoring systems, educational games,
etc. Knowledge tracking aims to assess the levels of
knowledge of students by observing a series of learning
activities. It helps to innovate human teaching management
techniques to improve learning efficiency and achieve
personalized learning. For example, the teaching system
can perceive the learning needs of students for providing
knowledge remediation and recommending personalized
courses [3]. Students recognize their own levels and devote
more energy to knowledge weaknesses. Educational experts
optimize the design of the teaching contents and methods

The knowledge tracking task can be expressed as a
model of the states S = {s1, s2, ..., st−1} of knowledge
of students by observing the sequence of past interactions
X = {x1, x2, ..., xt−1} of the students, and predicting the
possibility of correct answers pt (yt = 1|qt, X) to the new
exercise. Knowledge composition is a collective term for
skills, concepts, problems, etc., which is used to describe
knowledge. It is usually assumed that the learning process
can be divided into two stages: (1) After each exercise,
students learn and memorize related knowledge, and use
a set of variables to summarize the levels of the hidden
knowledge corresponding to the past exercise xt ∼f (ht).
(2) Students use the already learned knowledge to deal with
new exercises, and predict the performances on the new
exercises through the mastery of the hidden knowledge ht ∼
g (ht−1). Input data is the sequence of past interactions, and
each exercise is a tuple xt = (qt, rt), where qt is the question
at time t, and rt is the correct answer to the question, usually
binary.
However, it is inherently difficult to simulate the learning
process of humans with simple numerical values [5].
With the explosion of online education and the increasing
complexity of courses, complex knowledge components
and rich semantic relationship between coursework and
textbooks are encountered, which are collectively called
as the complex background knowledge. This complexity
poses two challenges to track knowledge accurately: (1)
Sparse curriculum problem: It exacerbates the uneven
distribution of interactions. For example, in order to avoid
boring repetition, pedagogy experts design a variety of
exercises, but all of them are not clicked by students. (2)
Insufficient diagnosis problem: It produces many invalid
diagnostic results. For example, since the mapping between
knowledge and exercises is broad, many unimportant or
difficult candidate exercises make learning suggestions
difficult to implement. The question is how to capture
the complex background knowledge for modeling the
proficiency accurately?
Traditional probability models mainly include Bayesian
Knowledge Tracing (BKT) [6, 7] and item response theory
(IRT) [8]. BKT [7] uses binary state variables to describe
knowledge mastery and use bayesian belief network
to model knowledge updating. Its mechanism of state
transition can be regarded as a simple noise binary channel,
which is parameterized by the possibility of guessing,
sliding, learning, and forgetting. On the other hand, IRT uses
a value vector to describe knowledge mastery, model the
interaction between the knowledge level and difficulty level
through an item reflection function, and to predict the future
performances of students. These early methods use strong
assumptions for decomposing the background knowledge
into a series of hierarchical knowledge components. Each
exercise uses one or more knowledge components, which
is conducive to the derivation of the sparse knowledge as
Fig. 1a. They rely on the concept of map predefined by
pedagogical experts. Under the condition of high-quality
expert experience, they can effectively estimate the levels of
mastery of students’ predefined knowledge, and explain the
results of knowledge tracking and the evolution process.
The latest research focuses on how to model the learn-
ing behaviors in large-scale complex historical interactions
through deep learning methods. A deep learning method
named DKT [5] integrates the modeling of the long-term
and short-term memory units (LSTM) [9] of knowledge
evolution in the recurrent neural network (RNN). The
knowledge state of a student is represented by one sum-
marized hidden high-dimensional continuous vector, which
encode the temporal behavior of information gathered in
the past as Fig. 1b. Since the LSTM modeling unit is a
nonlinear complex function, it is more flexible than affine
transformation and can capture subtle differences in com-
plex interactions. Although DKT substantially improves the
predicting performance compared with the earlier proba-
bilistic models, it is difficult to find specific patterns and
track their evolutions. Dynamic Key-Value Memory Net-
works(DKVMN) [10] uses an external storage matrix, based
on Memory-Augmented Neural Networks(MANN), to rep-
resent knowledge and extend DKT. The key matrix stores
the correlations between exercises and concepts. The value
matrix stores the correlations between the evolving exer-
cises and the states of knowledge as Fig. 1c. The knowledge
evolution is modeled by the “read and write” process. Since
the memory matrix can increase capacity, update parts, and
save relationships, it can model specific concepts gained
from the recent exercises in long sequences. In general, the
present KT research rarely discusses the important influence
of complex background knowledge, and mainly focuses on
the complex learning behaviors of humans. Deep learn-
ing methods have limited interpretability, and RNN-based
methods, such as DKT and DKVMN, have poor general-
ization effects in dealing with sparse courses [11]. In short,
the complex background knowledge poses new challenges
to knowledge tracking. Traditionally, only human super
teachers can handle such scenarios.

background knowledge can be regarded as a graph, called
